# Projective varieties

This page covers Chapter 4 of Fulton's *Algebraic Curves* [@Fulton2008], "Projective Varieties", pp. 43–52:

- §4.1 Projective space (pp. 43–44): $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$, homogeneous coordinates, the standard affine pieces $U_i$ and charts $\varphi_i$, the hyperplane at infinity.
- §4.2 Projective algebraic sets (pp. 44–48): zeros of polynomials on $\mathbb{P}^n$, homogeneous ideals, the affine cone, the projective Nullstellensatz, hypersurfaces, the homogeneous coordinate ring, function fields and local rings of projective varieties, projective changes of coordinates, and the linear-algebra problems 4.11–4.18.
- §4.3 Affine and projective varieties (pp. 48–50): homogenization and dehomogenization of ideals, the projective closure (Proposition 3), and the identification of function fields and local rings through affine charts.
- §4.4 Multiprojective space (pp. 50–51): biforms, algebraic sets in $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$ and in mixed spaces $\mathbb{P}^{n_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{P}^{n_r} \times \mathbb{A}^m$, and the Segre embedding (Problem 4.28).

The chapter rests on Chapters 1 and 2, described on the page [Affine algebraic sets and varieties](affine-varieties.md): the affine Nullstellensatz, affine algebraic sets and their ideals, and in particular §2.6 (forms, and the homogenization $f^*$ and dehomogenization $F_*$ of polynomials, Proposition 5 of §2.6, which is the milestone "Homogenization and dehomogenization of polynomials" there). Because the libraries' support for homogenization is central here, its status is recorded on this page as well (see Conventions).

Parts of this page: library status; conventions (the field, indexing, forms, zeros, homogenization, irreducibility, and the classical versus scheme-level notions, with advice on how the plane-curve library should use each); ten milestones grouped by section; the correspondence table; notes on the source; what was not made a milestone.

## Conventions

### The field

- Fulton's §4.1 explicitly allows **any field** $k$ for projective space and its charts; Problem 4.3(a) asks to check this. Problem 4.2 assumes $k$ **infinite**.
- From §4.2 on, $k$ is tacitly **algebraically closed**: the chapter relies on the convention stated at the start of Chapter 2 ("from now on" $k$ is a fixed algebraically closed field). The 1969 preface says instead that $k$ is algebraically closed from Chapter 3 on; for this chapter the difference does not matter, since §4.2 onwards uses the Nullstellensatz.
- **Characteristic:** nothing in Chapter 4 assumes characteristic zero, and Appendix A (nonzero characteristic) mentions no result of Chapter 4. Every milestone below holds in all characteristics.
- Many results need less than algebraic closure; each milestone says so. Purely algebraic statements (homogeneous ideals, graded quotients, homogenization of ideals) hold over any field, mostly over any commutative ring, which is how Mathlib and Tau Ceti state them.

### Indexing and coordinates

- Fulton writes $k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$, charts $U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}$ for $1 \le i \le n+1$, $\varphi_i(a_1,\dots,a_n) = [a_1 : \dots : a_{i-1} : 1 : a_i : \dots : a_n]$, and usually works with $U_{n+1}$ and $H_\infty = V(X_{n+1})$.
- In Lean the coordinates are `Fin (n + 1)`, numbered from 0. Fulton's $X_{n+1}$ is `X (Fin.last n)`. Tau Ceti's chart at `i : Fin (n + 1)` inserts the coordinate 1 at position `i` (`Fin.insertNth i 1`) and enumerates the other coordinates by `i.succAbove`; for `i = Fin.last n` this is $(a_1,\dots,a_n) \mapsto [a_1 : \dots : a_n : 1]$, exactly Fulton's $\varphi_{n+1}$.
- For the plane, $X, Y, Z$ are `X 0`, `X 1`, `X 2` in `MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k`; Fulton's line at infinity $Z = 0$ is `V(X 2)`, his chart $U_3$ is Tau Ceti's `Projectivization.affineOpen 2`, and $F_* = F(X, Y, 1)$ is `TauCeti.dehomogenize 2 F : MvPolynomial (Fin 2) k`. Tau Ceti's plane-curve files already follow this convention.

### Forms, gradings and zeros

- A *form of degree $d$* is a homogeneous polynomial. Mathlib's `MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous φ d` makes $0$ homogeneous of every degree, and forms of degree $d$ make up the submodule `MvPolynomial.homogeneousSubmodule σ R d`. Fulton's "nonconstant form" must be written as homogeneous of positive degree and nonzero.
- The grading of `MvPolynomial σ R` by total degree is `MvPolynomial.gradedAlgebra`, which is **not a global instance**; files using `Ideal.IsHomogeneous`, `HomogeneousIdeal` or `HomogeneousLocalization` for polynomial rings add `attribute [local instance] MvPolynomial.gradedAlgebra`, as Tau Ceti does.
- Fulton says $P$ is a zero of an arbitrary polynomial $F$ when $F$ vanishes at **every** set of homogeneous coordinates of $P$. For a form one representative suffices; for a general $F$ and infinite $k$ this means that every homogeneous component of $F$ vanishes at $P$ (Problem 4.2). Over a finite field the two readings differ. Tau Ceti's (complex-only) projective zero locus uses Fulton's every-representative convention.

### Homogenization and dehomogenization of polynomials

Fulton (§2.6) defines $F_* = F(X_1, \dots, X_n, 1)$ for forms $F$ and $f^* = X_{n+1}^d f(X_1/X_{n+1}, \dots, X_n/X_{n+1})$ with $d = \deg f$. Library support, checked in both libraries:

- **Mathlib** has only the one-variable case: `Polynomial.homogenize p n : MvPolynomial (Fin 2) R` (Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Homogenize.lean) homogenizes $p \in R[X]$ to a prescribed degree $n$; the original variable becomes `X 0` and the homogenizing variable `X 1`. It has `Polynomial.homogenize_mul` (for degrees bounded by the prescribed ones), `Polynomial.isHomogeneous_homogenize`, `Polynomial.aeval_homogenize_X_one`. There is no multivariate homogenization or dehomogenization in Mathlib.
- **Tau Ceti** has the multivariate operations, in three separate places:
  - `TauCeti.dehomogenize i : MvPolynomial (Fin (n + 1)) R →ₐ[R] MvPolynomial (Fin n) R` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Dehomogenization.lean), defined for every polynomial and every coordinate `i`, with `TauCeti.aeval_dehomogenize`, `TauCeti.dehomogenize_injOn` (a form is determined by its degree and its dehomogenization), `TauCeti.dehomogenize_ne_zero_of_isHomogeneous`, and `TauCeti.exists_isHomogeneous_dehomogenize_eq` (every polynomial of total degree at most $N$ is the dehomogenization of a form of degree $N$; an existence statement, not a function).
  - `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize b d` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/BlockHomogenize.lean): homogenization of a polynomial in variables `S` to prescribed degrees `d h`, one homogenizing variable per block `h : B`, in the variables `B ⊕ S` (homogenizing variables on the left); terms exceeding the degree bound are discarded. With `B = Unit` it is multivariate homogenization to a fixed degree; with several blocks it is multihomogenization (§4.4). It has additivity (`MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize_add`), dehomogenization back (`MvPolynomial.aeval_blockHomogenize_one_X`), homogeneity (`MvPolynomial.isWeightedHomogeneous_blockHomogenize`), the converse `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize_aeval_one_X`, and comparison with Mathlib (`MvPolynomial.rename_blockHomogenize_unique`). It has **no multiplicativity lemma** and is not connected to `TauCeti.dehomogenize`.
  - `MvPolynomial.weightHomogenization R w n` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/WeightFiltration.lean), $X^v \mapsto X^v t^{\,n - w(v)}$ with values in `(MvPolynomial σ R)[X]` (the new variable $t$ is the `Polynomial` variable); it uses truncated subtraction, so it behaves as a homogenization only on polynomials of weight at most `n`. With all weights `w = 1` it is homogenization to degree `n`, and it is the only multivariate homogenization in either library with a **multiplicativity lemma**: `TauCeti.MvPolynomial.weightHomogenization_mul` (for $p$ of weight $\le m$ and $q$ of weight $\le n$, the degree-$(m+n)$ homogenization of $pq$ is the product of the homogenizations; note the namespace `TauCeti.MvPolynomial`, while the definition is in the root `MvPolynomial` namespace), together with `MvPolynomial.eval_one_weightHomogenization` ($t = 1$ recovers $p$). It is built for Rees algebras and is not connected to `TauCeti.dehomogenize` or to polynomial rings in `Fin (n + 1)` variables.
- **Traps.** (1) Fulton homogenizes to the degree of $f$; the libraries homogenize to a prescribed degree $d$. With $d$ fixed, Proposition 5(2) of §2.6 loses its power of $X_{n+1}$: a form $F$ of degree $d$ is the degree-$d$ homogenization of $F_*$ (Tau Ceti's `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize_aeval_one_X`). Fulton's $f^*$ is the homogenization to degree `f.totalDegree`. (2) Variable conventions disagree: `TauCeti.dehomogenize i` works on `Fin (n + 1)` with `Fin.insertNth`, `blockHomogenize` on `Unit ⊕ Fin n` with the new variable first, Mathlib's `Polynomial.homogenize` on `Fin 2` with the new variable last. A library should fix one convention, matching `TauCeti.dehomogenize` and the charts, and prove a `rename` lemma for the other.

### Irreducibility

Fulton calls an algebraic set irreducible if it is not a union of two smaller algebraic sets, which makes $\emptyset$ irreducible; and his prime ideals (§1.1, p. 2) need not be proper, so $I(\emptyset) = R$ counts as prime and "irreducible iff $I(V)$ prime" (p. 45) and Problem 4.4 are correct in his conventions. Mathlib's conventions differ on both counts: `IsIrreducible` requires nonemptiness and `Ideal.IsPrime` requires $I \neq R$. Use Mathlib's, and add the hypotheses $V \neq \emptyset$ and $I \neq R$ where the book's statements need them. In the projective case there is a further exception: the irrelevant ideal $(X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})$ is a homogeneous prime ideal (in either convention) with empty zero set.

### Classical versus scheme-level projective geometry, and advice

Fulton's notions are **classical**: over an algebraically closed $k$, $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ is a set of points, algebraic sets are zero sets of forms, varieties are irreducible algebraic sets, and $\Gamma_h(V)$, $k(V)$, $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ are rings attached to such sets. The libraries contain both kinds of object:

- **Classical projective space.** Mathlib's `Projectivization K V` (notation `ℙ K V`; Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/) over any division ring, with `Projectivization.mk`, `Projectivization.rep`, linear subspaces, independence, the `PGL` action. Tau Ceti adds the standard charts (`Projectivization.affineOpen`, `Projectivization.affineChart`), change of field (`RingHom.mapProjective`), the Veronese map, and **complex-only** projective zero loci (`TauCeti.complexProjectiveZeroLocus`). Neither library has projective zero loci over a general field, ideals of subsets of $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$, cones, the Zariski topology on $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$, or classical multiprojective space.
- **Schemes.** Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj 𝒜` for an `ℕ`-graded ring, with `ProjectiveSpectrum.zeroLocus`, the charts `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.awayι`, stalks `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.stalkIso`, and properness. Tau Ceti builds projective schemes on it: `Proj I.quotientGrading` for a homogeneous ideal `I` of `R[X₀, …, Xₙ]` (proper, covered by the charts `D₊(Xⱼ)`, each identified with `Spec` of the dehomogenized quotient by `HomogeneousIdeal.awayEquivDehomogenize`), the scheme-level projective closure (`Ideal.homogenization`, `Ideal.homogenizationAwayEquiv`), `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IsProjectiveOver`, the plane curve `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve hG = Proj (R[X₀, X₁, X₂] ⧸ (G))` with its function field, and a homogeneous Nullstellensatz on `Proj` (`ProjectiveSpectrum.mem_radical_of_zeroLocus_subset`). Scheme points are relevant homogeneous primes, including non-closed points; nilpotents are allowed; the base is any ring.

The dictionary between the two, over algebraically closed $k$: points of $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ are the closed points of `Proj k[X₀, …, Xₙ]` (Tau Ceti proves the harder direction for `Proj (Sym N)`: `ProjectiveSpectrum.exists_ne_zero_forall_mem_symmetricAlgebra_iff_of_isClosed`); $V_p(I)$ is the set of closed points of `Proj (k[X] ⧸ I)`; $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ is the stalk at the closed point (`AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.stalkIso` identifies the stalk with `HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime`, which is literally Fulton's definition); Fulton's chart isomorphism $\alpha\colon k(V^*) \to k(V)$ comes, by passing to fraction fields, from the coordinate-ring isomorphism $(\Gamma_h)_{(x_i)} \cong k[Y]/I_*$ that is `HomogeneousIdeal.awayEquivDehomogenize`; the projective closure $V^*$ corresponds to `Proj (k[X] ⧸ Jʰ)`.

One point deserves emphasis. Fulton's *projective plane curve* (Chapter 5) is a form up to a nonzero scalar, so it may have multiple components. That is the closed subscheme `Proj (k[X, Y, Z] ⧸ (F))`, which Tau Ceti's `projectivePlaneCurve` is, and **not** the point set $V(F)$, a reduced object. The projective *varieties* of this chapter are reduced, irreducible point sets.

Advice for the plane-curve library:

1. **Points and charts.** Use `ℙ k (Fin (n + 1) → k)` for $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ (for the plane, `ℙ k (Fin 3 → k)`) and Tau Ceti's `Projectivization.affineChart`. Assume `[IsAlgClosed k]` only where a statement needs it, and where convenient state results with equations over $k$ and points over an algebraically closed extension $K$, as Mathlib's Nullstellensatz and Tau Ceti's plane-curve files do.
2. **A thin classical layer.** Define the projective zero locus of a set or ideal of polynomials over any field (generalizing Tau Ceti's complex-only `TauCeti.complexProjectiveZeroLocus` and its API, whose statements do not depend on ℂ; note that it asks the polynomials to vanish on every vector of the line, the origin included, which agrees with Fulton's convention over an infinite field but not over a finite one), the vanishing ideal as a `HomogeneousIdeal`, and the Zariski topology on `ℙ k (Fin (n + 1) → k)`. Then irreducibility, closures and irreducible components come from Mathlib's topology (`IsIrreducible`, `closure`, `irreducibleComponents`, `TopologicalSpace.NoetherianSpace`), and most of Proposition 3 of §4.3 becomes general topology (see the milestone on projective closure). Do the same on the affine side, in agreement with the [affine page](affine-varieties.md).
3. **Local rings.** Define $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ as `HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime` of the graded ring $\Gamma_h(V)$ at the homogeneous prime of $P$. This is Fulton's definition word for word, it is a local ring in Mathlib (`HomogeneousLocalization.isLocalRing`), and it *is* the scheme stalk (`AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.stalkIso`), so one object serves both worlds. In particular $\mathcal{O}_P(\mathbb{P}^2)$, where Chapter 5 computes intersection numbers, is `AtPrime` of `MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k`.
4. **Bridges, not duplication.** Keep the classical point-set notions for what Fulton does with points (intersection numbers, Bézout, linear systems), and use Tau Ceti's scheme-level results (properness, smoothness, genus, function fields of plane curves) through two bridge lemmas that neither library has: the bijection between $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ and the closed points of `Proj k[X₀, …, Xₙ]` (restricting to $V_p(I)$ and the closed points of `Proj (k[X] ⧸ I)`), and the identification of the classical local ring with the stalk, which is immediate with the definition in item 3.
5. **Multiprojective space** (§4.4) is used later in the book for products of varieties and the Segre embedding (Chapter 6) and for blowing up and nonsingular models (Chapter 7). Build only what those chapters need; the milestones below give the statements and the library tools.

## Milestones

### §4.1 Projective space

> [!milestone] Standard affine charts of projective space
> Let $k$ be any field and $n \ge 0$, and let $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ be the set of lines through the origin of $k^{n+1}$, equivalently the nonzero vectors of $k^{n+1}$ up to nonzero scalars.
>
> (a) Whether the $i$-th homogeneous coordinate is zero is well defined, and so are the ratios $x_j/x_i$ when $x_i \neq 0$. The set $U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}$ is in bijection with $\mathbb{A}^n$ through $\varphi_i$, which inserts a $1$ in position $i$, with inverse given by the ratios (the nonhomogeneous coordinates).
>
> (b) $\mathbb{P}^n = U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_{n+1}$.
>
> (c) For $n \ge 1$, $H_\infty = \mathbb{P}^n \setminus U_{n+1}$ is in bijection with $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ by dropping the last (zero) coordinate; for $n = 0$, $H_\infty = \emptyset$ and $\mathbb{P}^0$ is a single point.
>
> (d) $\mathbb{P}^1(k)$ is $\mathbb{A}^1$ together with the single point $[1:0]$.
>
> (e) (Problem 4.3) For a subfield $k' \subseteq k$, $\mathbb{P}^n(k')$ embeds in $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ by viewing coordinates in $k$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.1, pp. 43–44, Examples (0)–(2), Problem 4.3].
>
> Proof routes: direct. Nothing here needs more than a field; (a), (b) and (e) hold over a division ring.
>
> Formalization notes: Use `ℙ k (Fin (n + 1) → k)`. Fulton's $\varphi_i$ is `(Projectivization.affineChart i).symm`; `affineChart i` is a `PartialEquiv` (over a field, `[Field K]`) whose source is `Projectivization.affineOpen i` and whose target is all of `Fin n → K`, so the symm has source `univ` and target `affineOpen i`. For (c), use `Projectivization.map` of the linear injection $k^n \to k^{n+1}$ that appends a zero coordinate, and show its image is the complement of `affineOpen (Fin.last n)`; this is missing. For (d), Mathlib's `OnePoint.equivProjectivization` already identifies `OnePoint k` with `ℙ k (Fin 2 → k)`. The same charts are the open embeddings for the Zariski topology used in the milestone on projective closure. Examples (3) and (4) of the book (parallel lines meet at infinity; asymptotes) are illustrations, not milestones.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: `Projectivization`, `Projectivization.mk`, `Projectivization.rep`, `Projectivization.mk_eq_mk_iff'`, `Projectivization.map` (Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/Basic.lean) and `OnePoint.equivProjectivization` (Mathlib/Topology/Compactification/OnePoint/ProjectiveLine.lean); no charts. Tau Ceti **partial**: (a), (b) **full** through `Projectivization.affineOpen`, `Projectivization.mk_mem_affineOpen_iff`, `Projectivization.iUnion_affineOpen`, `Projectivization.affineChart`, `Projectivization.affineChart_mk`, `Projectivization.affineChart_symm_apply` (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/AffineChart.lean); (e) **full** through `RingHom.mapProjective`, `RingHom.mapProjective_injective` (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/BaseChange.lean); $\mathbb{P}^0$ through `Projectivization.subsingleton_of_rank_le_one` (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/Basic.lean); (d) through `OnePoint.equivProjectivization_mem_affineOpen_one_iff` (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/OnePoint.lean); (c) —.

### §4.2 Projective algebraic sets

> [!milestone] Homogeneous ideals and graded quotients of the polynomial ring
> Let $k$ be a field and $R = k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$, graded by total degree.
>
> (a) (Proposition 1) An ideal $I \subseteq R$ is homogeneous (it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements) if and only if it is generated by forms; a homogeneous ideal is then generated by finitely many forms.
>
> (b) (Problem 4.4) A homogeneous ideal $I \neq R$ is prime if and only if, for all forms $F, G$, $FG \in I$ implies $F \in I$ or $G \in I$.
>
> (c) (Problem 4.5) The radical of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous. (Not in the book, but used tacitly: sums, products and intersections of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous.)
>
> (d) (Proposition 2) For a homogeneous ideal $I$, every element of $\Gamma = R/I$ is uniquely a finite sum $f_0 + f_1 + \cdots$ with $f_d$ the residue of a form of degree $d$: $\Gamma$ is the direct sum of its pieces $\Gamma_d$, a graded $k$-algebra.
>
> (e) (Problem 4.9) Each $\Gamma_d$ is a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space.
>
> (f) (Problem 4.10) For a nonzero form $F$ of degree $m \ge 1$ in $R = k[X, Y, Z]$ and $\Gamma = R/(F)$: multiplication by $F$ gives an exact sequence $0 \to R_{d-m} \to R_d \to \Gamma_d \to 0$ (with $R_e = 0$ for $e < 0$), and $\dim_k \Gamma_d = dm - m(m-3)/2$ for every $d \ge m$ (indeed for every $d \ge \max(0, m-2)$; it fails for $d = m - 3 \ge 0$). The book states this for $F$ irreducible and $\Gamma = \Gamma_h(V(F))$, which is $R/(F)$ exactly when $F$ has no repeated factor.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.2, Proposition 1 (p. 45), Proposition 2 (p. 46), Problems 4.4, 4.5, 4.9, 4.10 (p. 47)].
>
> Proof routes: the book's. For (a), compare homogeneous components in a combination of generating forms; finiteness comes from the Hilbert basis theorem applied to a finite generating set, whose homogeneous components generate. For (f), multiplication by $F$ is injective because $R$ is a domain, and $\dim R_d = \binom{d+2}{2}$.
>
> Formalization notes: Use Mathlib's graded API for `MvPolynomial (Fin (n + 1)) k` with `MvPolynomial.gradedAlgebra` as a local instance: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous (homogeneousSubmodule _ k)` and `HomogeneousIdeal`. For (d) use Tau Ceti's `HomogeneousIdeal.quotientGrading` (the induced grading of the quotient, an `abbrev`, with a global `GradedAlgebra I.quotientGrading` instance); a "form of degree $d$ in $\Gamma$" is an element of `I.quotientGrading d` (again $0$ is a form of every degree). Fulton requires $k$ algebraically closed throughout, but (a)–(f) hold over any field, and (a)–(d) over any commutative ring (Noetherian for the finiteness in (a)). Fulton's (f) assumes $F$ irreducible and $d > m$; for $\Gamma = R/(F)$ only $F \neq 0$ is used (so that multiplication by $F$ is injective), and the formula also holds for $d = m, m-1, m-2$: for $d \ge m$, $\dim \Gamma_d = \binom{d+2}{2} - \binom{d-m+2}{2} = dm - m(m-3)/2$, and the polynomial $(d-m+2)(d-m+1)/2$ also vanishes at $d = m-1, m-2$, where $R_{d-m} = 0$. Problem 4.4 is used in the proofs that irreducible sets have prime ideals and of Proposition 3(3) of §4.3. The bigraded version needed in §4.4 comes from the same Mathlib API with weights in `ℕ × ℕ` (see the multiprojective milestone).
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: (a) without finiteness: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.iff_exists`, `Ideal.homogeneous_span` (Mathlib/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Homogeneous/Ideal.lean), `MvPolynomial.mem_iff_homogeneousComponent_mem` (Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Homogeneous.lean); (b) **full**: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_iff`, `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_of_homogeneous_mem_or_mem` (Mathlib/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Radical.lean); (c) **full**: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical`, `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.sup`, `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.inf`, `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.mul`; (e) for $R$ itself: `MvPolynomial.homogeneousSubmodule_fg`. Tau Ceti **partial**: finiteness in (a) through `MonomialOrder.exists_finset_isGroebnerBasis_isWeightedHomogeneous` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/Homogeneous.lean) with `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.ideal_eq_span` (Groebner/Basic.lean); (d) **full**: `TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.isInternal_quotientPiece`, `TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.gradedAlgebraQuotientPiece`, `HomogeneousIdeal.quotientGrading` (TauCeti/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Homogeneous/Quotient.lean), `TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.quotientPiece` (TauCeti/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Quotient.lean); (f) ingredients: `TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.finrank_quotientPiece_span_singleton_add` (TauCeti/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/HilbertSeries/RegularElement.lean) and `TauCeti.finrank_homogeneousSubmodule_eq_multichoose` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Finrank.lean), not yet combined.

> [!milestone] Projective algebraic sets, their ideals and affine cones
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed (for (a)–(c) an infinite field suffices) and $R = k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$. A point $P \in \mathbb{P}^n$ is a zero of $F \in R$ if $F$ vanishes at every set of homogeneous coordinates of $P$. For $S \subseteq R$ let $V(S) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be the common zeros, and for $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ let $I(X)$ be the polynomials vanishing on $X$.
>
> (a) (Problem 4.2) If $F$ vanishes at every set of homogeneous coordinates of $P$, so does each homogeneous component of $F$. Hence $V(S)$ equals $V$ of the ideal generated by $S$, and equals $V$ of finitely many forms (projective algebraic sets).
>
> (b) (Problem 4.6) $I(X)$ is a homogeneous radical ideal, and the projective analogues of properties (1)–(10) of §§1.2–1.3 hold: $V$ and $I$ reverse inclusions; $V(\sum_\alpha I_\alpha) = \bigcap_\alpha V(I_\alpha)$; $V(I) \cup V(J) = V(IJ) = V(I \cap J)$; $V(0) = \mathbb{P}^n$ and $V(1) = \emptyset$; $I(\emptyset) = R$ and $I(\mathbb{P}^n) = 0$; a point $[a]$ is $V$ of the linear forms $a_iX_j - a_jX_i$, which generate $I(\{[a]\})$; $V(I(X))$ is the smallest algebraic set containing $X$, and $V(I(V)) = V$ for algebraic $V$.
>
> (c) (Cone) For a projective algebraic set $V$ let $C(V) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ be the union of the lines in $V$ together with the origin. If $V \neq \emptyset$, the affine ideal of $C(V)$ equals $I(V)$; if $I$ is homogeneous with $V(I) \neq \emptyset$, then $C(V(I))$ is the affine zero set of $I$.
>
> (d) A nonempty algebraic set $V$ is irreducible if and only if $I(V)$ is prime. Every projective algebraic set is, uniquely, a finite irredundant union of projective varieties (its irreducible components).
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.2, pp. 44–46, Problems 4.2 (p. 44) and 4.6 (p. 47)].
>
> Proof routes: the book's, as in the affine case. (a): for fixed coordinates $x$, $\lambda \mapsto F(\lambda x) = \sum_i \lambda^i F_i(x)$ is a polynomial vanishing on the infinite set $k^\times$. (d): Problem 4.4 (starred) and Noetherianity of $R$; the book's development of $V(S)$ uses Problem 4.2 (starred), which is item (a) here.
>
> Formalization notes: Define the zero locus for an ideal (or set) of `MvPolynomial (Fin (n + 1)) k` with values in `Set (ℙ k (Fin (n + 1) → k))`; prove early that for a homogeneous ideal $I$ and $v \neq 0$, $[v] \in V(I)$ iff $v$ lies in Mathlib's affine `MvPolynomial.zeroLocus k I`. > [!milestone] Projective Nullstellensatz
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $n \ge 0$, $R = k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$, and $I \subseteq R$ a homogeneous ideal.
>
> (1) $V(I) = \emptyset$ if and only if $I$ contains all forms of degree at least $N$ for some $N$, if and only if $(X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})^N \subseteq I$ for some $N$, if and only if $\operatorname{Rad}(I) \supseteq (X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})$.
>
> (2) If $V(I) \neq \emptyset$, then $I(V(I)) = \operatorname{Rad}(I)$.
>
> (3) Consequently $V \mapsto I(V)$ is a bijection from projective algebraic sets onto the homogeneous radical ideals other than $(X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})$ (with $\emptyset \mapsto R$), restricting to a bijection from projective varieties onto the homogeneous prime ideals other than $(X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})$.
>
> (4) Hypersurfaces. Let $n \ge 1$. Every form of positive degree has a zero in $\mathbb{P}^n$. If $F = \lambda F_1^{e_1} \cdots F_r^{e_r}$ with $\lambda \in k^\times$ and pairwise non-associate irreducible forms $F_i$, then $I(V(F)) = (F_1 \cdots F_r)$. Hence $F \mapsto V(F)$ is a bijection from forms of positive degree without repeated factors, up to nonzero scalars, onto hypersurfaces of $\mathbb{P}^n$; irreducible forms correspond to irreducible hypersurfaces. Hyperplanes are the zero sets of linear forms; the coordinate hyperplanes are $V(X_i)$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.2, p. 46 (the projective Nullstellensatz is unnumbered; the hypersurface correspondence is in the paragraph after it)].
>
> Proof routes: the book's, through the cone: for homogeneous proper $I$, $V(I) = \emptyset$ iff the affine zero set of $I$ in $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ is contained in $\{0\}$, and the affine Nullstellensatz together with Problem 2.41 (starred: a finitely generated ideal inside the radical of an ideal has a power inside that ideal) gives (1); (2) is the affine Nullstellensatz for the cone. (4) follows from (2) and unique factorization, using that the irreducible factors of a form are forms. A scheme-level route (Tau Ceti's homogeneous Nullstellensatz on `Proj`, plus density of closed points) is possible but longer; the cone route is the natural one.
>
> Formalization notes: State it in Mathlib's generality: $I$ an ideal of `MvPolynomial (Fin (n + 1)) k`, zeros in `ℙ K (Fin (n + 1) → K)` for an algebraically closed extension $K$ of $k$; this matches `MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical`, which already has this form, and Tau Ceti's habit of equations over $k$ with points over $\bar k$. "All forms of degree at least $N$" is Mathlib's `MvPolynomial.mem_pow_idealOfVars_iff` for `MvPolynomial.idealOfVars`. Problem 2.41 is `Ideal.exists_pow_le_of_le_radical_of_fg`. Depends on the milestone on projective algebraic sets (cone) and on the affine Nullstellensatz ([affine page](affine-varieties.md)). Part (4) is the bridge to Chapter 5, where a projective plane curve is a form up to scalars (possibly with repeated factors) and its point set is $V(F)$.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical` (affine, Mathlib/RingTheory/Nullstellensatz.lean), `Ideal.exists_pow_le_of_le_radical_of_fg` (Mathlib/RingTheory/Finiteness/Ideal.lean), `MvPolynomial.idealOfVars`, `MvPolynomial.mem_pow_idealOfVars_iff` (Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Ideal.lean), `MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.funext` (two forms of the same degree with the same values over an infinite field are equal). > [!milestone] Homogeneous coordinate ring, function field and local rings of a projective variety
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ a nonempty projective variety, with homogeneous coordinate ring $\Gamma_h(V) = R/I(V)$ (a graded domain) and fraction field $k_h(V)$.
>
> (a) The set $k(V)$ of quotients $f/g$ of forms $f, g \in \Gamma_h(V)$ of the same degree with $g \neq 0$ is a subfield of $k_h(V)$ containing $k$; such a quotient defines a function where $g \neq 0$. $\Gamma_h(V) \not\subseteq k(V)$.
>
> (b) For $P \in V$, the set $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ of $z \in k(V)$ that can be written $f/g$ with $g(P) \neq 0$ is a local subring of $k(V)$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_P(V)$, the $z$ admitting such a representation with $f(P) = 0$; the value $z(P) = f(x)/g(x)$, for any coordinates $x$ of $P$, is well defined.
>
> (c) (Problem 4.17) For $z \in k(V)$, the pole set $\{P \in V : z \notin \mathcal{O}_P(V)\}$ is an algebraic subset of $V$.
>
> (d) A linear change of coordinates $T$ of $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ induces a bijection of $\mathbb{P}^n$ (a projective change of coordinates). For an algebraic set $V = V(F_1, \dots, F_r)$, $V^T := T^{-1}(V) = V(F_1^T, \dots, F_r^T)$ with $F^T = F(T_1, \dots, T_{n+1})$; $V$ is a variety iff $V^T$ is; and $T$ induces isomorphisms $\Gamma_h(V) \cong \Gamma_h(V^T)$, $k(V) \cong k(V^T)$, and $\mathcal{O}_P(V) \cong \mathcal{O}_Q(V^T)$ whenever $T(Q) = P$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.2, pp. 46–47, Problem 4.17 (p. 48)].
>
> Proof routes: the book's; all routine. (c): the pole set is the zero set of the homogeneous ideal generated by the forms $g$ that occur as denominators of $z$.
>
> Formalization notes: Represent $k(V)$ as Mathlib's `HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime 𝒜 ⊥` for the grading `𝒜 = I.quotientGrading` (the fractions $f/g$ of equal degree with $g \neq 0$), and $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ as `HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime 𝒜 𝔭` for the homogeneous prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of forms vanishing at $P$; both match Fulton's definitions exactly, the second is local by `HomogeneousLocalization.isLocalRing`, and it is the stalk of the scheme `Proj Γ_h(V)` at $\mathfrak{p}$ by `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.stalkIso`. Still missing: that `AtPrime 𝒜 ⊥` is a field for a graded domain with a nonzero element of positive degree, the injective maps $\mathcal{O}_P(V) \to k(V)$ (Fulton treats $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ as a subring; in Lean it is a separate ring with an injective algebra map, or a `Subring` of $k(V)$), and the value map. For (d) use Mathlib's action of `PGL(ι, k)` on `ℙ k (ι → k)` and Tau Ceti's `MvPolynomial.linearSubst` (which satisfies $F^M(x) = F(Mx)$, `MvPolynomial.aeval_linearSubst`); a graded automorphism of $R$ preserving $I(V)$ induces the maps of homogeneous localizations. Chapter 5 uses this milestone for $V = \mathbb{P}^2$ and for curves.

> [!milestone] Linear subvarieties and projective changes of coordinates in projective space
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed (items (a)–(h) hold over any field, except that "$V$ is a variety" in (a) needs $k$ infinite: over a finite field every subset of $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$ is algebraic, so a linear subvariety with more than one point is reducible; (i) holds over any infinite field and fails over finite fields).
>
> (a) (Problem 4.11) A linear subvariety $V = V(H_1, \dots, H_r)$, $H_i$ linear forms, is carried to a linear subvariety by every projective change of coordinates; some change of coordinates $T$ gives $V^T = V(X_{m+2}, \dots, X_{n+1})$, so $V$ is a variety if nonempty; and $m$ does not depend on $T$ (the dimension of $V$, with $m = -1$ iff $V = \emptyset$). Equivalently, linear subvarieties of dimension $m$ are the projectivizations of the $(m+1)$-dimensional subspaces of $k^{n+1}$.
>
> (b) (Problem 4.12) Any $m \le n$ hyperplanes of $\mathbb{P}^n$ have a common point.
>
> (c) (Problem 4.13) For distinct points $P = [a]$, $Q = [b]$, the line through them is $\{[\lambda a + \mu b] : (\lambda, \mu) \neq (0, 0)\}$. Projective analogue of Problem 2.15: changes of coordinates carry lines through $P, Q$ to lines through the images; lines are exactly the linear subvarieties of dimension 1, each being the line through any two of its points; in $\mathbb{P}^2$ lines are hyperplanes; and (our reading of the analogue of 2.15(d)) two distinct lines through $P$ and two distinct lines through $P'$ can be moved onto each other by a change of coordinates sending $P$ to $P'$.
>
> (d) (Problem 4.14) Given $n+1$ points $P_1, \dots, P_{n+1}$ of $\mathbb{P}^n$ not on a hyperplane, and likewise $Q_1, \dots, Q_{n+1}$, some projective change of coordinates sends each $P_i$ to $Q_i$ (in $\mathbb{P}^2$: three non-collinear points).
>
> (e) (Problem 4.15) Two distinct lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ meet in exactly one point.
>
> (f) (Problem 4.16) Dually, given $n+1$ hyperplanes with no common point, and likewise $M_1, \dots, M_{n+1}$, some projective change of coordinates sends the first to the second (in $\mathbb{P}^2$: three non-concurrent lines).
>
> (g) (Problem 4.18) Sending the hyperplane $V(\sum a_i X_i)$ to $[a_1 : \dots : a_{n+1}]$ is a bijection from hyperplanes onto $\mathbb{P}^n$; writing $P^*$ and $H^*$ for the correspondences, $P^{**} = P$, $H^{**} = H$, and $P \in H$ iff $H^* \in P^*$.
>
> (h) (Problem 4.24) The lines of $\mathbb{P}^2$ through $[0:1:0]$ are the lines $V(X - \lambda Z)$, $\lambda \in k$, and the line at infinity $V(Z)$.
>
> (i) (Problem 4.25) For $P = [x:y:z]$, the coefficient vectors $(a, b, c)$ of lines through $P$ form a plane through the origin of $\mathbb{A}^3$; hence for any finite set of points of $\mathbb{P}^2$ some line passes through none of them.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.2, Problems 4.11–4.16 and 4.18 (p. 48); §4.3, Problems 4.24 and 4.25 (p. 50)].
>
> Proof routes: linear algebra in $k^{n+1}$: linear subvarieties are projectivized subspaces; (d) extends independent vectors to a basis; (b) is $\dim \ker \ge n + 1 - m \ge 1$; (i) uses that a vector space over an infinite field is not a finite union of proper subspaces. Fulton's hint for Problem 4.16 reduces it to Problem 4.14 (both starred) through the pairwise intersection points of the lines.
>

(g) is most naturally stated between `ℙ k V` and `ℙ k (Module.Dual k V)`; Mathlib's `Projectivization.orthogonal` and `Projectivization.cross` (for `Fin 3 → k`) give the dot-product version and a common point of two lines, which is the existence half of (e); uniqueness is not stated. ### §4.3 Affine and projective varieties

> [!milestone] Projective closure of affine algebraic sets
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and identify $\mathbb{A}^n$ with $U_{n+1} \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ through $\varphi = \varphi_{n+1}$.
>
> (a) Ideals. For an ideal $J \subseteq k[X_1, \dots, X_n]$ let $J^*$ be the ideal generated by the $F^*$, $F \in J$. It is homogeneous; a form $G$ lies in $J^*$ iff $G_* \in J$; $(J^*)_* = J$; $X_{n+1}G \in J^*$ implies $G \in J^*$; and $J$ prime implies $J^*$ prime. For a homogeneous ideal $I \subseteq k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$ let $I_*$ be the ideal of the $F_*$, $F \in I$. (Problem 4.19) If $J = (F)$ then $J^* = (F^*)$. (Problem 4.20) In general $J = (F_1, \dots, F_r)$ does not imply $J^* = (F_1^*, \dots, F_r^*)$: for the twisted cubic $V(Y - X^2, Z - X^3) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^3$, with homogenizing variable $W$, $ZW - XY \in I(V)^*$ but not in $((Y - X^2)^*, (Z - X^3)^*)$.
>
> (b) For an algebraic set $V \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$, its projective closure is $V^* = V(I(V)^*)$; for an algebraic set $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$, $V_* = V(I(V)_*) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$, which equals $\varphi^{-1}(V)$.
>
> (c) (Proposition 3) (1) $\varphi(V) = V^* \cap U_{n+1}$ and $(V^*)_* = V$. (2) $V \subseteq W$ implies $V^* \subseteq W^*$, and likewise for $(\cdot)_*$. (3) $V$ irreducible implies $V^*$ irreducible. (4) The irreducible decomposition $V = \bigcup V_i$ gives the irreducible decomposition $V^* = \bigcup V_i^*$. (5) $V^*$ is the smallest algebraic set of $\mathbb{P}^n$ containing $\varphi(V)$. (6) If $\emptyset \neq V \subsetneq \mathbb{A}^n$, no component of $V^*$ lies in or contains $H_\infty$. (7) If no component of $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ lies in or contains $H_\infty$, then $V_* \subsetneq \mathbb{A}^n$ and $(V_*)^* = V$.
>
> (d) For every nonzero $F \in k[X_1, \dots, X_n]$, $V(F)^* = V(F^*)$.
>
> (e) (Problems 4.21, 4.22) If $V \subseteq W \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ are varieties and $V$ is a hypersurface, then $W = V$ or $W = \mathbb{P}^n$. In particular a variety containing $H_\infty$ is $\mathbb{P}^n$ (with $V_* = \mathbb{A}^n$) or $H_\infty$ (with $V_* = \emptyset$).
>
> (f) $V \mapsto V^*$ and $W \mapsto W_*$ are mutually inverse bijections between nonempty affine varieties in $\mathbb{A}^n$ and projective varieties in $\mathbb{P}^n$ not contained in $H_\infty$.
>
> (g) (Problem 4.23) The subvarieties of $\mathbb{P}^1$ are its points and $\mathbb{P}^1$; those of $\mathbb{P}^2$ are its points, the curves $V(F)$ with $F$ an irreducible form, and $\mathbb{P}^2$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.3, Proposition 3 (pp. 48–49), the text after it (p. 49), Problems 4.19–4.23 (pp. 49–50); §2.6, Proposition 5 (p. 24) for $F^*$ and $F_*$].
>
> Proof routes: The book manipulates ideals with Proposition 5 of §2.6 and uses the affine Nullstellensatz in (7). A cleaner route for Lean: give $\mathbb{P}^n$ the Zariski topology and prove two lemmas, (i) $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism onto the open set $U_{n+1}$, whose complement is $V(X_{n+1})$ (Fulton states this as Problem 6.4 in Chapter 6; nothing here depends on that chapter), and (ii) the closure of $\varphi(V)$ is $V(I(V)^*)$, which is the book's proof of (5). Then (1), (3), (4) are general topology (closure of the image of a closed set under an open embedding, `IsIrreducible.closure`, closure of a finite union), and (7) follows because a nonempty open subset of an irreducible space is dense. The book's proof of (3) uses Problem 4.4 (starred) tacitly, the remark $V(F)^* = V(F^*)$ rests on Problem 4.19 (starred), and Problem 4.22 (starred) on Problem 4.21. Only (6), (e) and (g) need specific algebra: (6) uses $I(H_\infty) = (X_{n+1})$ (true over infinite $k$); (e) uses that a nonzero prime ideal contained in $(F)$, with $F$ irreducible, equals $(F)$ (unique factorization); (g) also uses the classification of the irreducible algebraic subsets of $\mathbb{A}^2$ (§1.6, Corollary 2, for infinite $k$) and, for $\mathbb{P}^2$, (e).
>
> Formalization notes: Tau Ceti's `Ideal.homogenization J i` is defined as the largest homogeneous ideal whose dehomogenization lies in $J$ (the homogeneous core of the comap along `TauCeti.dehomogenize i`). It **equals Fulton's $J^*$** for `i = Fin.last n`: each $F^*$ is a form with $(F^*)_* = F$, and a form $G$ with $G_* \in J$ is $X_{n+1}^r (G_*)^*$; this equality should be recorded in the library. > [!milestone] Affine charts preserve function fields and local rings
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed.
>
> (a) Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ be an affine variety and $V^* \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ its projective closure. For a form $f \in \Gamma_h(V^*)$, represented by a form $F$, the residue $f_* \in \Gamma(V)$ of $F_*$ does not depend on $F$. The map $\alpha \colon k(V^*) \to k(V)$, $f/g \mapsto f_*/g_*$ ($f, g$ forms of the same degree, $g \ne 0$), is a well-defined isomorphism of fields over $k$, and for $P \in V$ (viewed in $V^*$ through $\varphi_{n+1}$) it restricts to an isomorphism $\mathcal{O}_P(V^*) \cong \mathcal{O}_P(V)$.
>
> (b) Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ be a projective variety. For each $i$, $\varphi_i$ maps $V_*$ (formed with respect to $U_i$) bijectively onto $V \cap U_i$, and for $P \in V \cap U_i$ the local rings $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ and $\mathcal{O}_{\varphi_i^{-1}(P)}(V_*)$ are isomorphic. If $V \not\subseteq V(X_i)$, the degree-zero part of $\Gamma_h(V)$ localized at $x_i$ is $\Gamma(V_*)$, and $I(V)_* = I(V_*)$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.3, p. 49 (unnumbered)].
>
> Proof routes: the book's: direct verification with Proposition 5 of §2.6. A route through existing library code: Tau Ceti's `HomogeneousIdeal.awayEquivDehomogenize` identifies the degree-zero part of $\Gamma_h(V)$ localized at $x_i$ with $k[Y]/I(V)_*$; with $I(V)_* = I(V_*)$ (from the proof of Proposition 3(7)) this is $\Gamma(V_*)$. For local rings, either localize this isomorphism at the prime of $P$, or go through schemes: $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ is the stalk of `Proj Γ_h(V)` at $P$ (`AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.stalkIso`), the chart `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj.awayι` is an open immersion, so this stalk is the stalk of `Spec` of the chart ring, the localization of $\Gamma(V_*)$ at the maximal ideal of $P$.
>
> Formalization notes: This is what lets Chapter 5 compute local invariants of a projective curve in an affine chart ("$\mathcal{O}_P(F)$ is $\mathcal{O}_{(x,y)}(F_*)$ when $P = [x:y:1]$"), so state (b) for every chart `i : Fin (n + 1)`, not just $U_{n+1}$, and make the isomorphism explicit on fractions ($p/X_i^m \mapsto p_*$, as in `HomogeneousIdeal.awayEquivDehomogenize_mk`). Fulton then identifies $k(V)$ with $k(V^*)$ and $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$ with $\mathcal{O}_P(V^*)$; in Lean keep the isomorphisms explicit. Depends on the milestone on projective closure.
>

### §4.4 Multiprojective space

> [!milestone] Algebraic sets in multiprojective and mixed spaces
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $n_1, \dots, n_r, m \ge 0$, and let $S$ be the polynomial ring in blocks of variables $X^{(j)} = (X^{(j)}_1, \dots, X^{(j)}_{n_j+1})$, $j = 1, \dots, r$, and $Y_1, \dots, Y_m$. A polynomial is multihomogeneous of multidegree $(d_1, \dots, d_r)$ if it is a form of degree $d_j$ in each block $X^{(j)}$ (no condition on the $Y$). For $r = 2$, $m = 0$ these are Fulton's biforms of bidegree $(p, q)$.
>
> (a) Every $F \in S$ is uniquely a finite sum of multihomogeneous components. An ideal is multihomogeneous iff generated by multihomogeneous elements (finitely many suffice); primality can be tested on multihomogeneous elements; the radical of a multihomogeneous ideal is multihomogeneous.
>
> (b) A multihomogeneous $F$ vanishes at a point of $\mathbb{P}^{n_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{P}^{n_r} \times \mathbb{A}^m$ at one set of coordinates iff at all. Algebraic sets $V_b(S)$, ideals $I_b(X)$ (multihomogeneous and radical), irreducibility ($V \neq \emptyset$ irreducible iff $I_b(V)$ prime), and decomposition into irreducible components behave as in the milestone on projective algebraic sets. (The Nullstellensatz needs a modified statement; see Notes on the source.)
>
> (c) For a variety $V$: the multihomogeneous coordinate ring $\Gamma_b(V)$, its fraction field $k_b(V)$, the function field $k(V)$ of quotients of multihomogeneous elements of equal multidegree, and local rings $\mathcal{O}_P(V)$, with the properties of the milestone on function fields and local rings. (Problem 4.27) Pole sets of rational functions are algebraic.
>
> (d) (Problem 4.26) With the charts $\varphi \colon \mathbb{A}^{n_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{A}^{n_r} \times \mathbb{A}^m \to U_{n_1+1} \times \cdots \times U_{n_r+1} \times \mathbb{A}^m$ (and the other choices of charts $\varphi_{i_1, \dots, i_r}$), the multiprojective closure of an affine algebraic set satisfies the analogue of Proposition 3 of §4.3, and $V \mapsto$ closure gives a bijection between nonempty affine varieties in $\mathbb{A}^{n_1 + \cdots + n_r + m}$ and varieties in the mixed space meeting $U_{n_1+1} \times \cdots \times U_{n_r+1} \times \mathbb{A}^m$, preserving function fields and local rings.
>
> (e) Affine, projective and multiprojective varieties are special cases ($\mathbb{A}^0$ is a point).
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.4, pp. 50–51, Problems 4.26 and 4.27 (p. 51)].
>
> Proof routes: the book leaves the whole development to the reader, by analogy with §§4.2–4.3. The multiprojective closure is the composite of the projective closures in each block; the multihomogenization of a polynomial is the block homogenization.
>
> Formalization notes: Points as products `ℙ k (Fin (n₁ + 1) → k) × ⋯ × (Fin m → k)`. Multidegrees are best handled by Mathlib's weighted gradings: put all variables in one type (for $r = 2$, `Fin (n + 1) ⊕ Fin (m + 1)`) with weights in `ℕ × ℕ` (or `Fin r → ℕ`; the affine variables get weight zero), and use `MvPolynomial.IsWeightedHomogeneous`, `MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousComponent` and the graded algebra `MvPolynomial.weightedGradedAlgebra`. Trap: Mathlib's primality and radical criteria for homogeneous ideals (Mathlib/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Radical.lean) require a linearly ordered cancellative grading monoid, which `ℕ × ℕ` with the product order is not; grade by the lexicographic product `ℕ ×ₗ ℕ` (Mathlib provides its ordered cancellative monoid structure in Mathlib/Algebra/Order/Monoid/Prod.lean) or `Lex (Fin r → ℕ)`. Tau Ceti's `MvPolynomial.IsMultiHomogeneous` uses variables `κ × ι`, all blocks of the same size, which does not fit $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$ with $n \ne m$; its `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize` allows blocks of any sizes and is the right tool for the closure in (d). Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj` takes `ℕ`-graded rings only, so there is no scheme-level multiprojective `Proj`; scheme-level products are fibre products of schemes. Fulton cites Problem 4.26 in Chapter 6 (§§6.2–6.3: affine open pieces of varieties in multispaces, local rings and regular functions); Problem 4.27 is not cited later, while its projective case, Problem 4.17, is used in Chapter 8.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `MvPolynomial.IsWeightedHomogeneous`, `MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousComponent`, `MvPolynomial.weightedDecomposition`, `MvPolynomial.weightedGradedAlgebra` (Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/WeightedHomogeneous.lean). Tau Ceti **related**: `MvPolynomial.IsMultiHomogeneous` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/MultiHomogeneous.lean); `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize`, `MvPolynomial.isWeightedHomogeneous_blockHomogenize`, `MvPolynomial.blockHomogenize_aeval_one_X` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/BlockHomogenize.lean); finite multihomogeneous generators for any weight: `MonomialOrder.exists_finset_isGroebnerBasis_isWeightedHomogeneous` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/Homogeneous.lean). > [!milestone] Segre embedding of a product of two projective spaces
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed ((a)–(c) hold over any field), $n, m \ge 0$, $N = (n+1)(m+1) - 1$, with coordinates $X_0, \dots, X_n$ on $\mathbb{P}^n$, $Y_0, \dots, Y_m$ on $\mathbb{P}^m$ and $T_{ij}$ ($0 \le i \le n$, $0 \le j \le m$) on $\mathbb{P}^N$. Let $S([x], [y]) = [x_i y_j]_{i,j}$.
>
> (a) $S \colon \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m \to \mathbb{P}^N$ is well defined and injective.
>
> (b) For every algebraic $W \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N$, $S^{-1}(W)$ is algebraic in $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$.
>
> (c) The image of $S$ is $V = V(T_{ij}T_{kl} - T_{il}T_{kj} : 0 \le i, k \le n,\ 0 \le j, l \le m)$, and $S(U_i \times U_j) = V \cap U_{ij}$, where $U_{ij} = \{t_{ij} \ne 0\}$.
>
> (d) $V$ is a variety.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §4.4, Problem 4.28 (p. 51)].
>
> Proof routes: (a), (c): the matrix $(x_i y_j)$ has rank one, and a nonzero matrix has rank one iff all its $2 \times 2$ minors vanish, in which case it factors as $x y^{\mathsf T}$, unique up to scalars. (b): a form $G$ of degree $d$ in the $T_{ij}$ pulls back to the biform $G(x_i y_j)$ of bidegree $(d, d)$. (d): the kernel of $k[T] \to k[X, Y]$, $T_{ij} \mapsto X_i Y_j$, is prime (the target is a domain), its zero set is $V$ by (c), and the projective Nullstellensatz gives $I(V)$ equal to that kernel; alternatively, $V$ is the image of the irreducible space $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$ under a continuous map.
>
> Formalization notes: Index the target by `Fin (n + 1) × Fin (m + 1)` rather than by `Fin (N + 1)`; then $S([x], [y]) = [x y^{\mathsf T}]$ (Mathlib's `Matrix.vecMulVec`), and the quadrics are the $2 \times 2$ minors of the generic matrix, Mathlib's `MvPolynomial.determinantalIdeal k (Fin (n + 1)) (Fin (m + 1)) (Fin 2)`. The kernel in (d) is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.toricIdeal` for the exponents $e_i + f_j$ (`TauCeti.toricIdeal_eq_ker_aeval_monomial`), generated by binomials (`TauCeti.toricIdeal_eq_span`). Tau Ceti's Veronese map is a model for the classical part: `Projectivization.veronese`, `Projectivization.veronese_injective`, and (over ℂ only) the image as a zero locus, `Projectivization.range_veronese`. Fulton uses this problem in Chapter 6 to show that closed subvarieties of products of projective spaces are projective varieties, and suggests it in Chapter 7 for nonsingular models.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `MvPolynomial.determinantalIdeal` (Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/DeterminantalIdeal.lean), `Matrix.vecMulVec`. Tau Ceti **related**: `TauCeti.toricIdeal`, `TauCeti.toricIdeal_eq_ker_aeval_monomial`, `TauCeti.toricIdeal_eq_span` (TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/ToricIdeal/Basic.lean); `Projectivization.veronese`, `Projectivization.veronese_injective`, `Projectivization.range_veronese` (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Projectivization/Veronese.lean). ## Notes on the source

1. **Field.** §4.1 is over an arbitrary field; Problem 4.2 needs $k$ infinite; §§4.2–4.4 assume $k$ algebraically closed through the convention stated at the opening of Chapter 2. The 1969 preface places that convention at Chapter 3; within this chapter the difference is immaterial. No result of the chapter assumes characteristic zero, and Appendix A does not touch it.
2. **Numbering.** Propositions are numbered through the chapter, not within sections: Propositions 1 and 2 are in §4.2, Proposition 3 in §4.3; Fulton himself cites "Proposition 3 of §4.3" (Problem 4.26) and "Proposition 5 of §2.6". The projective Nullstellensatz is unnumbered (p. 46). Problems run 4.1–4.28 through the chapter. Starred: 4.2, 4.4, 4.9, 4.11–4.17, 4.19, 4.22–4.28. Unstarred: 4.1, 4.3, 4.5–4.8, 4.10, 4.18, 4.20, 4.21. Chapter 5 cites the unstarred Problem 4.18, and Chapter 6 cites the unstarred Problem 4.8.
3. **Misprint in §4.3 (p. 48).** In the definition of $V_*$ for an algebraic set $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$, the ideal $I(V)$ is said to lie in $k[X_1, \dots, X_n]$; it lies in $k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$. (Checked on the printed page.) (The text uses $I^*, V^*$ for homogenization and $I_*, V_*$ for dehomogenization, as the notation index confirms.)
4. **Misprints in §4.4 (pp. 50–51), all present on the printed pages.** The definition of $V(S) = V_b(S)$ reads "$\{(x,y) \mid \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m \mid F(x,y) = 0 \dots\}$", with a bar where "$\in$" is meant; the intended set is $\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m : F(x, y) = 0 \text{ for all } F \in S\}$. The function field of a biprojective variety is defined as a set of $z \in k_h(V)$ where $k_b(V)$, the fraction field of $\Gamma_b(V)$ defined just before, is meant. In Problem 4.26(b) (p. 51) the products $\mathbb{A}^{n_1} \times \mathbb{A}^{n_r}$ and $\mathbb{P}^{n_1} \times \mathbb{P}^{n_r}$ lack the dots for the middle factors (the later products in the same problem have them).
5. **Empty set and irreducibility.** As in §1.5, the definition makes $\emptyset$ irreducible, and $I(\emptyset) = R$ is prime in Fulton's sense (his prime ideals, p. 2, need not be proper), so "irreducible iff $I(V)$ prime" (p. 45) is correct as stated. In Mathlib's conventions it needs $V \neq \emptyset$ (the text itself later speaks of "a nonempty projective variety"). In either convention the irrelevant ideal is a homogeneous prime with $V = \emptyset$, so homogeneous primes correspond to varieties only after excluding it, as the book says ("we must always make an exception").
6. **Problem 4.4** is correct in Fulton's convention ($R$ counts as prime); with Mathlib's `Ideal.IsPrime` it needs the hypothesis $I \neq R$.
7. **Proposition 1** asserts a *finite* set of generating forms; the proof takes homogeneous components of a finite generating set, which exists by the Hilbert basis theorem (not mentioned).
8. **Proof of Proposition 3.** (5) begins with "if $F \in I(W)$" and applies $F_*$, which is defined only for forms; read "for a form $F \in I(W)$", which suffices because $I(W)$ is homogeneous. (3) uses Problem 4.4 tacitly. (6) uses $I(H_\infty) = (X_{n+1})$, which needs $k$ infinite. (7) reduces to irreducible $V$, which needs $(V \cup W)_* = V_* \cup W_*$; this follows from $V_* = \varphi_{n+1}^{-1}(V)$, which the book states only afterwards, for varieties and without proof ("the points on $V \cap U_i$ correspond to points on $V_*$", p. 49).
9. **Problem 4.10** assumes $F$ irreducible and $d > n$. For $\Gamma = k[X,Y,Z]/(F)$ the formula $\dim \Gamma_d = dn - n(n-3)/2$ holds for any nonzero form $F$ of degree $n \ge 1$ and all $d \ge \max(0, n - 2)$, and fails at $d = n - 3 \ge 0$ (where $\dim \Gamma_d = \binom{n-1}{2}$ but the formula gives $\binom{n-1}{2} - 1$). This $\Gamma$ is the book's $\Gamma_h(V(F))$ only when $F$ has no repeated factor: for $F = X^2$, $\Gamma_h(V(F)) = k[X,Y,Z]/(X)$ has $\dim \Gamma_d = d + 1$, not $2d + 1$.
10. **§4.4 and the Nullstellensatz.** Fulton asks the reader to repeat "the entire development" of §4.2 for $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$. Part (2) of the projective Nullstellensatz does not carry over verbatim. In $\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1$ with coordinates $X_0, X_1; Y_0, Y_1$, the bihomogeneous radical ideal $I = (X_0, X_1) \cap (Y_0) = (X_0 Y_0, X_1 Y_0)$ has $V_b(I) = \mathbb{P}^1 \times \{[0:1]\} \neq \emptyset$ but $I_b(V_b(I)) = (Y_0) \neq \operatorname{Rad}(I)$. The correct statement uses the irrelevant ideal $J = (X_i Y_j)_{i,j}$: $V_b(I) = \emptyset$ iff $\operatorname{Rad}(I) \supseteq J$, and in general (for bihomogeneous $I$, $k$ algebraically closed) $I_b(V_b(I))$ is the radical of the saturation $(I : J^\infty)$, which equals $\operatorname{Rad}(I) : J$. (Checked by hand: $V_b(I)$ is the image of the affine zero set of $I$ minus $V_a(J)$, and $I_a(V_a(I) \setminus V_a(J)) = \operatorname{Rad}(I) : J$.) Fulton states no multiprojective Nullstellensatz explicitly, and later chapters do not seem to need one; the library should state the saturated form if it needs one.
11. **The correspondence of hypersurfaces with forms** (p. 46) is stated without the hypothesis $n \ge 1$; for $n = 0$ it degenerates: every hypersurface of $\mathbb{P}^0$ is empty, so the correspondence is a bijection only trivially (the one squarefree nonconstant form $X_1$, up to scalars, gives $\emptyset$), and $I(V(F)) = R \neq (F)$.
12. **Problem 4.21** (p. 50) says "see Problem 1.30", which is about $\mathbb{R}$; the affine analogue it means is apparently Problem 1.39(b) (an irreducible hypersurface of $\mathbb{A}^n$ lies in no irreducible algebraic set other than itself and $\mathbb{A}^n$).
