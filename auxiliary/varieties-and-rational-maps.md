# Varieties, morphisms and rational maps

This page covers Chapter 6 of Fulton's *Algebraic Curves*, "Varieties, Morphisms, and Rational Maps" [@Fulton2008, pp. 67–80]: §6.1 The Zariski topology (pp. 67–69), §6.2 Varieties (pp. 69–70), §6.3 Morphisms of varieties (pp. 70–73), §6.4 Products and graphs (pp. 73–75), §6.5 Algebraic function fields and dimension of varieties (pp. 75–77) and §6.6 Rational maps (pp. 77–79), together with the remark on this chapter in Appendix A (p. 113).

Fulton develops here, in the classical language, general (quasi-projective) varieties: open subsets of irreducible algebraic sets in products of affine and projective spaces, with their rings of regular functions. The later plane-curve chapters (Chapter 7, resolution of singularities and nonsingular models; Chapter 8, the Riemann–Roch theorem) use only a small part of it: function fields in one variable, transcendence degree and dimension, rational maps and birational equivalence of curves, regular functions on affine open sets, and the fact that every curve has a plane model. This page is written so that the library takes that part, in a form built on function fields, and leaves the rest to Mathlib's schemes. It builds on [affine varieties](affine-varieties.md) (Chapters 1–2) and [projective varieties](projective-varieties.md) (Chapter 4).

Parts of the page:

1. Conventions, including a recommended representation: what to take from Mathlib and Tau Ceti, which thin classical layer to build, and what to skip.
2. Eight milestones: regular functions on principal open sets (§6.3); two generators, residue rings and dimension for function fields of one variable (§6.5); rational maps and function-field embeddings, birational equivalence, plane models, and maps between curves (§6.6).
3. The correspondence table: every definition, numbered result and starred problem of the chapter, with its status in Mathlib and Tau Ceti.
4. Notes on the source: errata, gaps, and an error in Appendix A (Proposition 9(2) does hold in characteristic $p$).
5. Deferred material.

## Conventions

### The field and its characteristic

- From Chapter 3 on, $k$ is a fixed algebraically closed field [@Fulton2008, Preface], so everything in Chapter 6 assumes it. It is used essentially in: Proposition 9(3) (the residue ring is $k$ itself, not merely algebraic over $k$), Proposition 10(3) (dimension zero means a point), and every identification of points with maximal ideals (Nullstellensatz), for instance in Proposition 5.
- The preface says that characteristic zero is assumed "whenever convenient", often without comment in problems, and that Appendix A treats arbitrary characteristic. In Chapter 6 characteristic zero is assumed explicitly in Proposition 9(2) and Problem 6.31∗ (primitive element theorem), and therefore implicitly in the Corollary to Proposition 12 (every curve is birational to a plane curve), whose proof uses Proposition 9(2), and in Problem 6.32∗ (which works in the setting of 6.31) and Problem 6.43∗(c) (which uses 6.32). Problem 6.36 says "assume char(k) = 0 if you wish". Appendix A (p. 113) states that Proposition 9(2) "is not true as stated in characteristic $p$" and observes that $x$ can be chosen with $K/k(x)$ separable, which is what the Corollary needs; the Corollary's own proof points to Appendix A for characteristic $p$. Appendix A's first claim is mistaken: Proposition 9(2) holds in every characteristic (see the Notes); only Fulton's proof needs characteristic zero.
- Every milestone below is stated for arbitrary characteristic unless it says otherwise.

### Fulton's varieties and the libraries' schemes

- A **variety** in Fulton (§6.2, p. 69) is an open subset $X$ (tacitly nonempty) of a nonempty irreducible algebraic set $V$ in a mixed space $\mathbb{P}^{n_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{P}^{n_r} \times \mathbb{A}^m$, with the induced Zariski topology. Fulton's note after Proposition 4 says these are the quasi-projective varieties. A variety is a set of $k$-points. Its function field is $k(X) = k(V)$, its local rings are $\mathcal{O}_P(X) = \mathcal{O}_P(V) \subseteq k(X)$, and for open $U$ the ring $\Gamma(U) = \bigcap_{P \in U} \mathcal{O}_P(X)$ is again a subring of $k(X)$: all rings of functions are subrings of one field, and restriction is inclusion.
- Mathlib and Tau Ceti work with schemes: Fulton's varieties correspond to integral separated schemes of finite type over $k$. The traps: a scheme has non-closed points, and Fulton's points are the closed points, which over algebraically closed $k$ are the $k$-points (`AlgebraicGeometry.pointEquivClosedPoint`); the local ring is the stalk `X.presheaf.stalk x`, and the sections `Γ(X, U)` are abstract rings that map injectively to the function field (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.germToFunctionField_injective`) rather than subrings of it; Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.functionField` is a `CommRingCat` with no $k$-algebra structure; for `X.Over (Spec k)` Tau Ceti supplies one (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.baseRingToFunctionField` and the instance `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.functionFieldBaseAlgebra`, `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/BaseAlgebra.lean`).
- A **closed subvariety** is irreducible by definition (§6.2); "subvariety" means open or closed subvariety.
- A **morphism** (§6.3) is a continuous map pulling regular functions back to regular functions; for varieties these are the morphisms of $k$-schemes. Where Fulton writes "homomorphism" between coordinate rings or function fields (Propositions 2 and 11(3)), a $k$-algebra homomorphism is meant (in Lean, `→ₐ[k]`): the opening of Chapter 2 (p. 17) fixes this convention for all rings containing $k$.

### Curves

- A **curve** in Fulton (§6.5, p. 75) is a variety of dimension one: irreducible, possibly affine, possibly singular. A **plane curve** (Chapters 3 and 5) is a polynomial up to a scalar, possibly reducible and with multiple components; Proposition 10(5) relates the two notions for irreducible polynomials.
- Tau Ceti's `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IsCurveOver k` (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/Basic.lean`) means integral, **proper** and of dimension one over $k$: it excludes affine curves, so it is not Fulton's "curve". Its `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.RegularProperCurveCat k` adds discrete valuation rings as local rings, which is Fulton's nonsingular projective curve of Chapter 7. Mathlib has no notion of a curve.
- Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve hG` is the scheme $\operatorname{Proj}(k[X_0, X_1, X_2]/(G))$ of a homogeneous $G$ (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/PlaneCurve/Basic.lean`).

### Function fields, transcendence degree and dimension

- Fulton defines $\operatorname{tr.deg}_k K$ as the least $n$ such that $K$ is algebraic over $k(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ for some $x_i$. Mathlib's `Algebra.trdeg k K` is a `Cardinal`, the supremum of the cardinalities of algebraically independent families. They agree for finitely generated $K$ by Problem 6.33, which Mathlib has (see the table). Compare with numerals or use `Cardinal.toNat`.
- An **algebraic function field in $n$ variables** is a finitely generated extension of transcendence degree $n$. For $n = 1$ use Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsFunctionField k K` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Basic.lean`): a proposition, not a class, saying that $K$ is finite over $k(x)$ for some transcendental $x$; for finitely generated $K$ it is equivalent to transcendence degree one (`TauCeti.isFunctionField_iff_trdeg_eq_one`). Avoid Mathlib's `FunctionField k K` for this purpose: it needs an instance `Algebra (RatFunc k) K`, that is, a chosen parameter.
- Fulton's $\dim X = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k k(X)$ is a natural number. Mathlib's Krull dimensions are `ringKrullDim : WithBot ℕ∞` and `topologicalKrullDim`. Tau Ceti bridges them: `TauCeti.ringKrullDim_eq_toNat_trdeg` and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.topologicalKrullDim_eq_toNat_trdeg_functionField`.
- Tau Ceti's function-field theory allows any base field and an inexact field of constants. With $k$ algebraically closed, $k$ is algebraically closed in $K$, so its exactness hypotheses hold automatically, and every place has degree one (`TauCeti.Place.degree_eq_one_of_isAlgClosed_of_isFunctionField`).

### Domination of local rings

- Fulton (§6.6, p. 77): for local rings $A \subseteq B$, $B$ **dominates** $A$ if $\mathfrak{m}_A \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_B$.
- Mathlib's `LocalSubring K` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean`) is partially ordered by domination, with **`A ≤ B` meaning that $B$ dominates $A$** ($A \subseteq B$ and the inclusion is a local homomorphism, i.e. $\mathfrak{m}_B \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_A$; for local rings this is equivalent to Fulton's condition). So "$\mathcal{O}_P(X)$ dominates $\widetilde{F}(\mathcal{O}_Q(Y))$" is `(O_Q).map F̃ ≤ O_P`. The order points from the dominated ring to the dominating one.
- `LocalSubring.ofPrime A P` realises the localization of a subring $A \subseteq K$ at a prime $P$ as a local subring of $K$: use it for $\mathcal{O}_P(C) \subseteq k(C)$. Discrete valuation rings enter through `ValuationSubring.toLocalSubring` and Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Place.integers`.

### Rational maps

- Fulton (§6.6): a rational map $X \dashrightarrow Y$ is an equivalence class of morphisms defined on nonempty open subvarieties; its domain is the union of their domains; it is **dominating** if a representative has dense image. Mathlib: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.PartialMap` (a morphism on a *dense* open subscheme), `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap` (notation `X ⤏ Y`), `Scheme.RationalMap.domain`, and `Scheme.RationalMap.IsDominant` ("dominant"). For irreducible $X$, nonempty open and dense open coincide.
- Fulton writes $\widetilde{F} : k(Y) \to k(X)$ for the contravariant map of a dominating rational map. Tau Ceti's name for a dominant morphism is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.functionFieldAlgHom k f`.
- A **birational map** is one represented by an isomorphism of open sets; Mathlib has `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.PartialIso` and `Scheme.Birational`. `Scheme.Birational` ignores any base scheme; to match Fulton's $k$-morphisms use `Scheme.BirationalOver` with the structure maps to $\operatorname{Spec} k$. A **rational variety** (birational to $\mathbb{A}^n$) is Mathlib's `Scheme.IsRationalOver` (which allows an affine space on an arbitrary index type; for a variety the index type is then finite of size $\dim X$).

### Recommended representation for the library

1. **Do not formalize Fulton's general varieties.** Neither the open subsets of irreducible algebraic sets in mixed spaces with the rings $\Gamma(U)$, nor §6.1–6.4 as a theory, is needed by Chapters 7–8. Those chapters need curves, and on a curve the proper closed subsets are the finite sets (Proposition 10(4)), so "nonempty open subset of a curve" can be read as "cofinite set of points". No Zariski topology needs to be built.
2. **Work inside the function field.** As in Fulton, all local rings of a curve are subrings of its function field. For an irreducible affine plane curve $F$: $\Gamma(F) = k[X, Y]/(F)$, $k(F) = \operatorname{Frac} \Gamma(F)$, and each $\mathcal{O}_P(F) \subseteq k(F)$ as a `LocalSubring` through `LocalSubring.ofPrime` (see [affine varieties](affine-varieties.md)). For an irreducible projective plane curve, take $k(F)$ and $\mathcal{O}_P(F)$ as advised on [projective varieties](projective-varieties.md): $k(F)$ is the field of quotients of forms of equal degree (`HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime 𝒜 ⊥`), and $\mathcal{O}_P(F)$ is the image in it of `HomogeneousLocalization.AtPrime 𝒜 𝔭`, a `LocalSubring` of $k(F)$; an affine chart containing $P$ identifies both with the affine ones. Then $\Gamma(U) := \bigcap_{P \in U} \mathcal{O}_P(F)$ for cofinite $U$ is a `Subalgebra` of $k(F)$ over $k$, built with `⨅` exactly as in Mathlib's `MaximalSpectrum.iInf_localization_eq_bot`.
3. **Use Tau Ceti's function-field library for the abstract side.** `TauCeti.IsFunctionField k K` for "function field of a curve"; the set `TauCeti.Place k K` of places, with their discrete valuation rings `P.integers`, as the points of the nonsingular model (Chapter 7). Tau Ceti proves these are exactly the proper valuation subrings of $K$ containing $k$ (`TauCeti.Place.ofValuationSubring`, `TauCeti.isDiscreteValuationRing_of_isFunctionField`), and its holomorphy rings `TauCeti.holomorphyRing S` $= \bigcap_{P \in S} \mathcal{O}_P$ are the rings $\Gamma(U)$ of the nonsingular model. If a scheme is wanted, Tau Ceti's anti-equivalence `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.RegularProjectiveCurveCat.equivFunctionFieldCat` connects to it.
4. **Maps between curves are field maps.** Define a dominating rational map $C' \dashrightarrow C$ to be a $k$-algebra map $k(C) \to k(C')$ (a morphism of `TauCeti.FunctionFieldCat k`), with its domain and values defined by domination (the fifth milestone), and birational equivalence to be a $k$-isomorphism of function fields (the sixth). A non-dominating rational map between curves is constant (the eighth).
5. **Dimension.** Use `Algebra.trdeg` for fields and Tau Ceti's Krull-dimension theorems for coordinate rings. Only dimensions zero and one are ever needed.
6. **Defer** products, graphs, the Segre embedding, mixed spaces, algebraic groups and general morphisms. If one is ever needed (for instance if the resolution page follows §7.3 literally, which uses morphisms into $\mathbb{P}^2 \times \mathbb{P}^1 \times \cdots \times \mathbb{P}^1$), take it from Mathlib's schemes (pullbacks, `AlgebraicGeometry.IsSeparated`, `Proj`) rather than building it classically.

## Milestones

### §6.3 Morphisms of varieties

> [!milestone] Regular functions on a principal open subset of an affine variety
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $V \subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ an affine variety with coordinate ring $\Gamma(V)$ and function field $k(V)$, and $f \in \Gamma(V)$, $f \neq 0$. Let $V_f = \{P \in V : f(P) \neq 0\}$ and $\Gamma(V_f) = \bigcap_{P \in V_f} \mathcal{O}_P(V) \subseteq k(V)$. Then:
>
> 1. $\Gamma(V_f) = \Gamma(V)[1/f] = \{a/f^m : a \in \Gamma(V),\ m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ inside $k(V)$.
> 2. $V_f$ is an affine variety: if $I = I(V)$ and $F \in k[X_1, \dots, X_n]$ has residue $f$, then $I' = (I, X_{n+1}F - 1) \subseteq k[X_1, \dots, X_{n+1}]$ is prime, and the projection $V(I') \to V_f$ is an isomorphism of varieties; on coordinate rings it is the isomorphism $\Gamma(V)[T]/(Tf - 1) \cong \Gamma(V)[1/f]$, $T \mapsto 1/f$.
> 3. (Corollary.) Every neighbourhood of a point $P$ of a variety contains an affine neighbourhood of $P$; in an affine variety it can be taken of the form $V_g$.
>
> For $f = 1$, part 1 is $\Gamma(V) = \bigcap_{P \in V} \mathcal{O}_P(V)$, Proposition 2 of §2.4 (see [affine varieties](affine-varieties.md)).
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.3, Proposition 5 and its Corollary, pp. 71–72]; [@Fulton2008, Problem 6.13∗].
>
> Proof routes: The book proves part 1 with the ideal $J$ of denominators of $z \in \Gamma(V_f)$: its zero set lies in $V(F)$, so $F^N \in J$ by the Nullstellensatz. Part 2 uses Problem 6.13∗ (the universal property of $R[1/f]$ and $R[1/f] \cong R[X]/(Xf - 1)$) and Problem 6.16∗ (restriction of morphisms), and leaves the last verification to the reader. The corollary picks $g$ vanishing on the complement and not at $P$ (Problem 1.17). An algebraic route suits Lean better: $R = \Gamma(V)$ is a finitely generated $k$-domain, so $R[1/f]$ is a Jacobson domain and equals the intersection of its localizations at maximal ideals (Mathlib's `MaximalSpectrum.iInf_localization_eq_bot`); the maximal ideals of $R[1/f]$ are the extensions of the maximal ideals of $R$ not containing $f$ (`IsLocalization.isMaximal_iff_isMaximal_disjoint`), which are the points of $V_f$; and $R[1/f]$ localized at $\mathfrak{m} R[1/f]$ is $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$.
>
> Formalization notes: State part 1 for a finitely generated domain $R$ over an algebraically closed field, with points the maximal ideals (or the $k$-algebra maps $R \to k$); the coordinate rings of irreducible affine plane curves are the case Chapter 7 uses (the neighbourhoods $C_h$ of §7.2, Step (3), and Lemma 2 of §7.5) and Chapter 8 uses (Problems 8.15 and 8.16). Define $\Gamma(U)$ for a cofinite set $U$ of points of a curve as an infimum of subalgebras, as in the recommended representation. Part 2 is needed only in the form "$\Gamma(V)[1/f] \cong \Gamma(V)[T]/(Tf - 1)$ and the points of $V_f$ are those of $V(I')$", which avoids defining morphisms of varieties. The corollary for a plane curve $C$: for $P \in C$ and a finite set $S$ with $P \notin S$ there is $h \in \Gamma(C)$ with $h(P) \neq 0$ and $h = 0$ on $S$.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: the scheme form, `AlgebraicGeometry.IsAffineOpen.isLocalization_basicOpen` and `AlgebraicGeometry.IsAffineOpen.basicOpen` (`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/AffineScheme.lean`); the case $f = 1$ as `MaximalSpectrum.iInf_localization_eq_bot` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Spectrum/Maximal/Localization.lean`); Problem 6.13∗ as `IsLocalization.Away.lift` and `Localization.awayEquivAdjoin` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/Away/Basic.lean`, `Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/Away/AdjoinRoot.lean`). Tau Ceti **related**: the analogue for normal curves with places as points, `TauCeti.coe_ofField_powers_eq_holomorphyRing` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/HolomorphyRing/Localization.lean`); it does not cover the non-valuation local rings of singular points.

### §6.5 Algebraic function fields and dimension of varieties

> [!milestone] Two-generator presentation of a function field of one variable
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $K$ an algebraic function field in one variable over $k$ (finitely generated, transcendence degree one).
>
> 1. (Proposition 9(1).) For every $x \in K \setminus k$, $K$ is algebraic, hence (being finitely generated) finite, over $k(x)$. Over an arbitrary field $k$ the same holds for every $x \in K$ transcendental over $k$.
> 2. (Proposition 9(2), in every characteristic.) For every $x \in K \setminus k$ there is $y \in K$ with $K = k(x, y)$. The book states and proves this in characteristic $0$ only, and Appendix A claims it fails in characteristic $p$; that claim is mistaken (see the Notes, which give the proof). This part states more than the book.
> 3. (Appendix A; any characteristic.) There is $x \in K \setminus k$ with $K/k(x)$ finite and separable, and for such $x$ the $y$ of part 2 is given by the primitive element theorem.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.5, Proposition 9(1),(2), p. 75]; [@Fulton2008, Appendix A, p. 113] for part 3; [@Fulton2008, Problem 6.31∗].
>
> Proof routes: Part 1 (book): $x$ is transcendental (Problem 1.48), and an algebraic relation between $x$ and a transcendence basis $t$ shows that $t$ is algebraic over $k(x)$. Part 2 for separable $K/k(x)$ (always so in characteristic $0$) and part 3: the primitive element theorem for the finite separable extension $K/k(x)$; the book proves it in characteristic zero in Problem 6.31∗ (starred). In part 3 the separating $x$ exists because $k$ is perfect. Part 2 for inseparable $K/k(x)$: the argument in the Notes (separable closure $S$ of $k(x)$ in $K$; $K = S(\alpha)$ because $[K : K^p] = p$; then the primitive element theorem with one separable generator).
>
> Formalization notes: State for `TauCeti.IsFunctionField k K`, with conclusion `k⟮x, y⟯ = ⊤`. Part 1 is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.finiteDimensional_adjoin` (hypothesis: $x$ transcendental). For part 3, and for part 2 when $K/k(x)$ is separable, apply `Field.exists_primitive_element k⟮x⟯ K` (it needs `FiniteDimensional` and `Algebra.IsSeparable`; in characteristic zero the latter comes from the instances `PerfectField.ofCharZero` and `Algebra.IsAlgebraic.isSeparable_of_perfectField`) and convert $k(x)(y)$ to $k(x, y)$. The separating element comes from `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.exists_transcendental_and_isSeparable_adjoin_of_perfectField`. Part 2 for an inseparable $K/k(x)$ is not reachable by Fulton's route ($k(x)$ is not perfect, and Mathlib's `Field.primitive_element_inf_aux` assumes the whole extension separable); it needs the three facts named in the Notes, none of which was found stated in either library. Nothing downstream needs it: the plane-model milestone and Chapter 8 can use a separating $x$. Used by the plane-model milestone below, by Chapter 8 (finiteness of $[K : k(x)]$ in §8.2, Proposition 4; the plane model in §8.4, Proposition 6).
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: the primitive element theorem `Field.exists_primitive_element` (`Mathlib/FieldTheory/PrimitiveElement.lean`); part 1 for Mathlib's chosen-parameter function fields as `FunctionField.finiteDimensional_of_adjoin_transcendental` (`Mathlib/NumberTheory/FunctionField.lean`). Tau Ceti **partial**: part 1 in full as `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.finiteDimensional_adjoin` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Basic.lean`); the separating element `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.exists_transcendental_and_isSeparable_adjoin_of_perfectField` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/SeparablyGenerated.lean`). Tau Ceti applies `Field.exists_primitive_element k⟮x⟯ F` inside proofs (for instance in `TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Consequences/SeparablePlace.lean`) but states no theorem $K = k(x, y)$.

> [!milestone] Residue rings of subrings of a function field of one variable
> Let $k$ be a field, $K$ an algebraic function field in one variable over $k$, and $R$ a $k$-subalgebra of $K$. If $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime ideal of $R$ with $0 \neq \mathfrak{p} \neq R$, then $R/\mathfrak{p}$ is algebraic over $k$; in particular it is a field and $\mathfrak{p}$ is maximal. If $k$ is algebraically closed, the natural map $k \to R/\mathfrak{p}$ is an isomorphism.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.5, Proposition 9(3), p. 75].
>
> Proof routes: The book's proof is elementary and suits Lean: given $x \in R$ and $0 \neq y \in \mathfrak{p}$, the elements $x, y$ are algebraically dependent (transcendence degree one); a nonzero relation $\sum_i a_i(x) y^i = 0$ of least degree has $a_0 \neq 0$ (otherwise divide by $y$), and $a_0(x) \in \mathfrak{p}$ shows that the residue of $x$ is algebraic over $k$. With $k$ algebraically closed, an element algebraic over $k$ lies in $k$ (Problem 1.48∗, applied to the fraction field of $R/\mathfrak{p}$). For finitely generated $R$ one can instead use dimension theory ($\dim R/\mathfrak{p} = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) = 0$); for valuation rings, Tau Ceti's degree bound for places.
>
> Formalization notes: Fulton assumes that $R$ is a domain with quotient field $K$; the proof needs only $R \subseteq K$, so state it for `R : Subalgebra k K` (or with `[Algebra R K]` injective). Uses: Proposition 10(4) (take $R = \Gamma(V)$), hence Chapter 7's Theorem 1; and, with $R$ a discrete valuation ring of $K$, the fact that residue fields of places are $k$, which lets Chapters 7–8 evaluate functions at places.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib —. Tau Ceti **partial**: the finitely generated case through `Ideal.ringKrullDim_quotient_eq_toNat_trdeg_residueField` and `TauCeti.ringKrullDim_eq_toNat_trdeg_of_isFractionRing` (`TauCeti/RingTheory/KrullDimension/FiniteType.lean`); the valuation-ring case as `TauCeti.Place.finiteDimensional_residueField` and `TauCeti.Place.degree_eq_one_of_isAlgClosed_of_isFunctionField` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Place/Degree.lean`). No statement for an arbitrary subring.

> [!milestone] Dimension of curves and of closed subvarieties of the plane
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and define $\dim X = \operatorname{tr.deg}_k k(X)$ for a variety $X$.
>
> 1. If $U$ is a nonempty open subvariety of $X$, then $\dim U = \dim X$.
> 2. If $V^*$ is the projective closure of an affine variety $V$, then $\dim V^* = \dim V$.
> 3. $\dim X = 0$ if and only if $X$ is a single point.
> 4. If $C$ is a curve, every closed subvariety of $C$ other than $C$ is a point. Hence the proper closed subsets of $C$ are its finite subsets, and its nonempty open subsets are its cofinite subsets. Algebraically: if $R$ is a finitely generated $k$-domain whose fraction field has transcendence degree one, every nonzero prime of $R$ is maximal.
> 5. A closed subvariety of $\mathbb{A}^2$ (resp. $\mathbb{P}^2$) has dimension one if and only if it is $V(F)$ for an irreducible polynomial (resp. irreducible form) $F$. Algebraically: the function field of an irreducible $F \in k[X, Y]$ has transcendence degree one, and the prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ of $k[X, Y]$ with $\operatorname{tr.deg}_k \operatorname{Frac}(k[X, Y]/\mathfrak{p}) = 1$ are exactly the ideals $(F)$ with $F$ irreducible.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.5, Proposition 10, pp. 75–76].
>
> Proof routes: The book: parts 1–2 because the function fields are equal; part 3 because $k(V)$ algebraic over $k$ forces $\Gamma(V) = k$ (Problem 2.4); part 4 from the residue-ring milestone above applied to $R = \Gamma(V)$ and the prime of $W$ (Problem 2.3); part 5 from the classification of the irreducible closed subsets of $\mathbb{A}^2$ (§1.6; see [affine varieties](affine-varieties.md)) and parts 3–4. In Lean, part 4 also follows from Tau Ceti's $\dim R = \operatorname{tr.deg} = 1$ and Mathlib's `Ideal.IsPrime.isMaximal_of_ne_bot`; part 5 from the dimension formula (a prime of $k[X, Y]$ with one-dimensional quotient has height one) and the fact that height-one primes of a factorial ring are principal.
>
> Formalization notes: Only dimensions zero and one matter for the library. With dimension defined through the function field, parts 1 and 2 are immediate once the projective closure is known to have the same function field (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve.functionFieldEquiv` does this for plane curves). Part 4 is what lets the library avoid the Zariski topology on curves. Part 5 is about irreducible $F$; Fulton's "plane curves" of Chapters 3 and 5 may be reducible. The book's proof of part 5 uses $\dim \mathbb{A}^2 = 2$ (Problem 6.34) silently.
>

### §6.6 Rational maps

> [!milestone] Dominating rational maps correspond to function-field embeddings
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $X, Y$ varieties.
>
> 1. A dominating rational map $F : X \dashrightarrow Y$ induces a $k$-algebra embedding $\widetilde{F} : k(Y) \to k(X)$: if $f : U \to V$ represents $F$ with $U \subseteq X$, $V \subseteq Y$ affine open, then $\widetilde{f} : \Gamma(V) \to \Gamma(U)$ is injective and extends to the function fields, independently of $f$.
> 2. For $P \in X$ and $Q \in Y$: $P$ lies in the domain of $F$ and $F(P) = Q$ if and only if $\mathcal{O}_P(X)$ dominates $\widetilde{F}(\mathcal{O}_Q(Y))$.
> 3. Every $k$-algebra homomorphism $k(Y) \to k(X)$ equals $\widetilde{F}$ for exactly one dominating rational map $F$.
>
> Curve form (what Chapters 7–8 use). Let $C, C'$ be curves, $\varphi : k(C) \to k(C')$ a $k$-algebra map, and $P' \in C'$. There is at most one $Q \in C$ such that $\mathcal{O}_{P'}(C')$ dominates $\varphi(\mathcal{O}_Q(C))$; the set of $P'$ for which such a $Q$ exists is cofinite in $C'$. If $C$ is affine with $\Gamma(C) = k[y_1, \dots, y_m]$, such a $Q$ exists if and only if $\varphi(y_i) \in \mathcal{O}_{P'}(C')$ for all $i$, and then $Q$ is the point with coordinates $\varphi(y_i)(P')$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.6, Proposition 11, p. 77]; definition of domination [@Fulton2008, §6.6, p. 77]; [@Fulton2008, Problems 6.24∗ and 6.26∗].
>
> Proof routes: The book: part 1 by Problem 6.26∗ (dense image gives an injective map of coordinate rings) and leaves the rest of part 1 and the forward half of part 2 to the reader. For the converse in part 2, take affine neighbourhoods $V \ni P$, $W \ni Q$ with $\Gamma(W) = k[y_1, \dots, y_n]$, write $\widetilde{F}(y_i) = a_i/b_i$ with $b_i(P) \neq 0$; then $\widetilde{F}(\Gamma(W)) \subseteq \Gamma(V_b)$ for $b = \prod b_i$ (the principal-open milestone above), so $\widetilde{F}$ comes from a morphism $V_b \to W$ (Proposition 2) sending $P$ to $Q$. Part 3 is the same construction on affine $X$, $Y$. In the curve form, uniqueness of $Q$ (when $C$ is projective) uses Problem 6.24∗: for $Q_1 \neq Q_2$ there is $g$ with $g \in \mathfrak{m}_{Q_1}$ and $1/g \in \mathcal{O}_{Q_2}$; cofiniteness holds because each $\varphi(y_i)$ has finitely many poles (the dimension milestone, part 4).
>
> Formalization notes: In the function-field library take part 3 as the definition: a dominating rational map $C' \dashrightarrow C$ *is* a map `k(C) →ₐ[k] k(C')`, and part 2 (in its curve form) *defines* its domain and its values. What must then be proved is the curve form: uniqueness, cofiniteness, and the affine description. Domination is Mathlib's `LocalSubring` order (direction as in the Conventions). For a projective plane curve $C$ work chart by chart. This is the classical layer under Chapter 7's Theorem 1 (a discrete valuation ring of $k(C')$ dominates a unique point of a projective curve), whose valuation-ring side Tau Ceti already has (`TauCeti.Place`). Depends on the principal-open milestone (Proposition 5) and on Proposition 1 of §2.2 (polynomial maps and algebra maps; see [affine varieties](affine-varieties.md)).
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap`, `Scheme.RationalMap.domain` and `Scheme.RationalMap.equivFunctionFieldOver` ($S$-rational maps $X \dashrightarrow Y$ correspond to $S$-morphisms $\operatorname{Spec} K(X) \to Y$, for integral $X$ and $Y$ locally of finite type over $S$; `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/RationalMap.lean`); `Scheme.RationalMap.IsDominant` (`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/Dominant.lean`); the domination order on `LocalSubring` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean`); Problem 6.26∗(b) in $\operatorname{Spec}$ form, `PrimeSpectrum.denseRange_comap_iff_ker_le_nilRadical` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Spectrum/Prime/Topology.lean`). Tau Ceti **partial**: part 1 for dominant morphisms, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.functionFieldAlgHom` (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/BaseAlgebra.lean`); part 3 when $X$ is integral with valuation rings as local rings and $Y$ is proper, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom` with `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.eq_homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom_iff` (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/AlgHom.lean`), which is the curve case of Chapter 7. Neither library has part 2, nor part 3 for arbitrary varieties.

> [!milestone] Birational equivalence is isomorphism of function fields
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed. Two varieties $X$ and $Y$ are birationally equivalent (there are nonempty open $U \subseteq X$, $V \subseteq Y$ and an isomorphism $U \cong V$) if and only if $k(X) \cong k(Y)$ as $k$-algebras; every $k$-isomorphism $k(Y) \to k(X)$ is $\widetilde{F}$ for a birational map $F$, which can be represented by an isomorphism between principal open subsets of affine open subsets. In particular a variety is birational to each of its nonempty open subvarieties, and $\mathbb{A}^n$ is birational to $\mathbb{P}^n$.
>
> Curve form. If $C, C'$ are curves and $\varphi : k(C) \to k(C')$ is a $k$-isomorphism, there are cofinite $U \subseteq C$, $U' \subseteq C'$ and a bijection $U' \to U$, $P' \mapsto Q$, with $\varphi(\mathcal{O}_Q(C)) = \mathcal{O}_{P'}(C')$ for every $P' \in U'$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.6, Proposition 12 and the definitions before it, p. 78].
>
> Proof routes: The book: reduce to affine $X$, $Y$; as in Proposition 11, $\varphi(\Gamma(X)) \subseteq \Gamma(Y_b)$ and $\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma(Y)) \subseteq \Gamma(X_d)$, so $\varphi$ restricts to an isomorphism between the coordinate rings of two principal open subsets, and Propositions 2 and 5 turn it into an isomorphism of varieties. The curve form follows from the curve form of the previous milestone applied to $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$: where both are defined and inverse to each other, mutual domination gives equality of the local rings.
>
> Formalization notes: For curves the library should *define* birational equivalence as `Nonempty (k(C) ≃ₐ[k] k(C'))`. Chapter 8's statement that birational curves have the same genus is then immediate, and this milestone supplies the geometric meaning used in Chapter 7 (isomorphic neighbourhoods of points under blowing up and quadratic transformations, §7.4 Step (5) and Problem 7.13) in the form "corresponding points have the same local ring". Depends on the principal-open milestone (§6.3) and on the previous milestone. With Mathlib, use `Scheme.BirationalOver` rather than `Scheme.Birational`.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **partial**: the definitions `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.PartialIso`, `Scheme.Birational`, `Scheme.BirationalOver`, `Scheme.IsRationalOver`, and "a dense open is birational to the whole", `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Opens.birational_of_dense` (`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/Birational.lean`); no criterion through function fields. Tau Ceti **partial**: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.isoOfFunctionFieldAlgEquiv` (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/AlgHom.lean`) turns a $k$-isomorphism of function fields into an isomorphism $X \cong Y$ when both schemes are integral, proper over $k$ and have valuation rings as local rings: the stronger statement for nonsingular projective curves (Chapter 7, Corollary 3 of §7.1), not Proposition 12.

> [!milestone] Every curve is birational to an affine plane curve
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, of any characteristic, and $K$ an algebraic function field in one variable over $k$ (for instance $K = k(V)$ for a curve $V$). There are $x, y \in K$ and an irreducible $F \in k[X, Y]$ such that $K = k(x, y)$ and the kernel of the $k$-algebra map $k[X, Y] \to K$, $X \mapsto x$, $Y \mapsto y$, is the ideal $(F)$. Hence $K \cong k(F) = \operatorname{Frac}(k[X, Y]/(F))$ over $k$, and $V$ is birationally equivalent to the affine plane curve $V(F)$ and to its projective closure. Any $x \in K \setminus k$ can be used (part 2 of the two-generator milestone; the book's own argument covers characteristic $0$, and Appendix A a separating $x$), and then $\deg_Y F = [K : k(x)]$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §6.6, Corollary to Proposition 12, p. 78]; [@Fulton2008, Appendix A, p. 113].
>
> Proof routes: The book: $K = k(x, y)$ by Proposition 9(2) (Appendix A in characteristic $p$); the kernel $I$ is prime, $V(I) \subseteq \mathbb{A}^2$ has function field $K$, so it has dimension one and is a plane curve by Proposition 10(5). A route better suited to Lean: let $G \in k(x)[Y]$ be the minimal polynomial of $y$; clearing denominators gives a primitive $F \in k[X][Y]$, irreducible in $k[X, Y]$ by Gauss's lemma; Tau Ceti's kernel lemma then gives $\ker = (F)$. Another: the kernel is a height-one prime of the factorial ring $k[X, Y]$, hence principal.
>
> Formalization notes: Suggested shape for `TauCeti.IsFunctionField k K`: there exist `x y : K` and `F : MvPolynomial (Fin 2) k` with `Irreducible F`, `k⟮x, y⟯ = ⊤` and `RingHom.ker (MvPolynomial.aeval ![x, y]) = Ideal.span {F}`, followed by the $k$-algebra isomorphism of $K$ with the fraction field of `MvPolynomial (Fin 2) k ⧸ Ideal.span {F}`. This is the entry point by which Chapter 7 (Theorem 3 of §7.5) reduces arbitrary curves to plane curves and Chapter 8 (§8.4, Proposition 6) gets affine coordinates. Depends on the two-generator milestone (through Problem 6.31∗) and, on the book's route, on part 5 of the dimension milestone.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: Gauss's lemma `Polynomial.IsPrimitive.irreducible_iff_irreducible_map_fraction_map` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/GaussLemma.lean`), `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.isPrincipal_of_height_eq_one` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/UFD.lean`). Tau Ceti **partial**: `TauCeti.ker_aeval_eq_span_singleton_of_irreducible` (the kernel is $(F)$ once an irreducible $F$ with $F(x, y) = 0$ and transcendental $x$ is given; `TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/PlaneCurve/ConstantField.lean`), and the converse direction `TauCeti.isFunctionField_of_aeval_eq_zero` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/PlaneCurve/Basic.lean`). > [!milestone] Rational maps between curves are constant or finite
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C$ and $C'$ curves, and $F : C' \dashrightarrow C$ a rational map.
>
> 1. Either $F$ is dominating, or $F$ is constant: there is $P \in C$ with $F(Q) = P$ for every $Q$ in the domain of $F$.
> 2. If $F$ is dominating, $k(C')$ is a finite extension of $\widetilde{F}(k(C))$.
>
> In function-field form: every $k$-algebra map between algebraic function fields of one variable over $k$ makes the target a finite extension of the image.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, Problem 6.45∗, p. 79].
>
> Proof routes: Part 1: the closure of the image of a representative is an irreducible closed subset of $C$, hence $C$ or a point (part 4 of the dimension milestone). Part 2: both fields have transcendence degree one, so $k(C')$ is algebraic over the image, and it is finitely generated, hence finite.
>
> Formalization notes: In the function-field representation, part 1 is the case split in the definition of a rational map between curves (a $k$-embedding of function fields, or a point of $C$); it needs a proof only if the library also has maps of curves given on points. Part 2 is a few lines from Tau Ceti and Mathlib (see Lean status). Used in Chapter 7 (Corollary 1 of Theorem 1: a discrete valuation ring of $k(C')$ cannot contain $\varphi(k(C))$, because $k(C')$ is finite over it) and Chapter 8 (finiteness of $[K : k(x)]$). Injectivity is automatic for maps of fields.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `Algebra.finite_of_essFiniteType_of_isAlgebraic` (`Mathlib/FieldTheory/IntermediateField/Adjoin/Algebra.lean`), the transcendence-degree tower law `trdeg_add_eq` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/AlgebraicIndependent/TranscendenceBasis.lean`). Tau Ceti **partial**: part 2 in substance, `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.isAlgebraic_of_isFunctionField` (algebraicity; finiteness then follows from `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.essFiniteType` and the Mathlib lemma above; `TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Basic.lean`), and the category of function fields with $k$-embeddings, `TauCeti.FunctionFieldCat` (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Category.lean`). Part 1 is not stated.

## Notes on the source

- **Numbering.** In this edition the propositions of Chapter 6 are numbered 1–12 consecutively through the chapter, not within sections: Proposition 1 is in §6.2, Propositions 2–5 in §6.3, 6–8 in §6.4, 9–10 in §6.5, 11–12 in §6.6. The three corollaries (after Propositions 5, 7 and 12) are unnumbered; this page calls them "Corollary to Proposition $n$". Problems are numbered 6.1–6.47. Fulton's own cross-reference in Chapter 7 (proof of Corollary 1 of §7.1, p. 82) cites "Proposition 11 of §6.5"; it is in §6.6.
- **Appendix A and Proposition 9(2).** Appendix A says that Proposition 9(2) ("$K = k(x, y)$ for every $x \in K \setminus k$") "is not true as stated in characteristic $p$". This is mistaken: the statement holds for every perfect $k$, in particular for Fulton's algebraically closed $k$; only Fulton's *proof* needs characteristic zero (or a separating $x$). Proof, using standard field theory: let $S$ be the separable closure of $k(x)$ in $K$, so $S/k(x)$ is finite separable and $K/S$ purely inseparable. (i) $[K : K^p] = p$: for a separating transcendental $t$ (which exists as $k$ is perfect), $K = K^p(t)$ because $K/k(t)$ is separable (a finite separable $L/F$ has $L = FL^p$) and $k \subseteq K^p$; and $t \notin K^p$, for otherwise $K = K^p$ and $t$ would have $p^n$-th roots in $K$ for every $n$, contradicting $[K : k(t)] < \infty$. Hence $[K : SK^p] \le p$. (ii) A finite purely inseparable extension $K/S$ with $[K : SK^p] \le p$ is simple: if $K \ne S$, pick $\alpha \in K \setminus SK^p$; then $K = S(\alpha)K^p$, and an extension $K/M$ that is finite purely inseparable with $K = MK^p$ is trivial (iterate to $K = MK^{p^n}$ and use $K^{p^n} \subseteq M$ for large $n$), so $K = S(\alpha)$. (iii) $S = k(x)(\beta)$ by the primitive element theorem, so $K = k(x)(\alpha, \beta)$ with $\beta$ separable over the infinite field $k(x)$; the classical argument (choose $c \in k(x)$ with $\alpha' + c\beta' \ne \alpha + c\beta$ for all conjugates $\alpha'$ of $\alpha$ and $\beta' \ne \beta$ of $\beta$, then $\gcd(\mathrm{minpoly}_\beta(T), \mathrm{minpoly}_\alpha(\gamma - cT)) = T - \beta$ for $\gamma = \alpha + c\beta$) needs only $\beta$ separable and gives $K = k(x)(\gamma)$. Fulton's own outline in Problem 6.31∗ is this argument, with separability used only for the first generator. Example: $K = k(t)$, $x = t^p$ gives $K = k(x, t)$ although $K/k(x)$ is purely inseparable. The two-generator milestone states the result in every characteristic on the strength of this proof; it was not found in either library.
- **Appendix A, typo.** "and then $k = k(x, y)$ for some $y$" should read "$K = k(x, y)$".
- **$k$-algebra homomorphisms.** Proposition 2 and Proposition 11(3) speak of "homomorphisms" of coordinate rings and function fields. This is not a gap: the opening of Chapter 2 (p. 17) declares that all rings contain $k$ and that "homomorphism" means a ring homomorphism fixing $k$. A formalization must keep this, since a bare ring map $k(Y) \to k(X)$ need not be $k$-linear when $k$ has nontrivial automorphisms (for instance $k = \mathbb{C}$).
- **§6.6, p. 77.** "For any point $P$ in the domain of $f$, the value $f(P)$ is well-defined in $k$" should say "in $Y$".
- **Proposition 5.** In (1), $\{a/f^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is the same set as with $n \in \mathbb{N}$, so harmless; likewise in Problem 6.13. In the proof of (2), "If $W$ is closed in $V$, defined by polynomials $G_\beta(X_1, \dots, X_{n+1})$" should read "closed in $V'$"; the proof that $\varphi^{-1}$ is a morphism is left to the reader.
- **Proposition 9(3).** Fulton assumes that $R$ has quotient field $K$; the proof uses only $R \subseteq K$. In the proof, "$a_0(x) \in P$" should be "$\in \mathfrak{p}$", and the conclusion uses Problem 1.48∗ (algebraically closed $k$), which is stated for fields and must be applied to the fraction field of $R/\mathfrak{p}$. In the proof of Proposition 10(4), "$\Gamma(W) = R/P\,\mathfrak{p}$" is a typo for $R/\mathfrak{p}$.
- **Problem 6.12.** "a neighborhood $U$ of $z$" should be "of $P$".
- **Problem 6.31.** In the outline, "$H(x_i) \neq 0$ if $i > 0$" should be "$i > 1$", and "$G = \prod (T - y_i)$" should have index $j$. The choice of $\lambda$ and the gcd step use that the roots $x_i$ of $F$ are distinct, which is where characteristic zero (separability of $x$) enters; separability of $y$ is never used.
- **Proofs left to the reader or sketched.** Proposition 2 (details), Proposition 3 (entirely, with its multiprojective generalization), Proposition 4 (only the chart computation is given), Proposition 7(1), Proposition 11(1) and the forward half of 11(2), the last step of Proposition 5(2). The proof of Proposition 10(5) uses $\dim \mathbb{A}^2 = 2$ (Problem 6.34) without comment, and the Corollary to Proposition 12 uses $I = I(V(I))$ for the prime kernel $I$ (Nullstellensatz).
- **Starred problems that later chapters rely on.** Chapter 7's Theorem 1 and its Corollary 1 use Problems 6.24∗, 6.25∗ and 6.45∗ (and Proposition 10(4) and Proposition 11); Chapter 7's Theorem 3 uses Problem 6.43∗ and the Corollary to Proposition 12; Chapter 8 uses Proposition 9(1) and the Corollary to Proposition 12. Within Chapter 6, Proposition 1 uses Problems 6.7∗ and 6.8∗, Proposition 5 uses 6.13∗ and 6.16∗, Proposition 6 uses 6.20∗, Proposition 7 uses 6.14∗ and 6.16∗, Proposition 9(2) uses 6.31∗, and Proposition 11 uses 6.26∗.

## Deferred

These are out of scope for the plane-curve library, which needs curves only and represents them through their function fields:

- **§6.1 beyond curves**: the Zariski topology on mixed spaces and Problems 6.3∗, 6.4∗, 6.6∗ in general. On a curve the topology is the cofinite one, so no topology needs to be built. If needed, Mathlib's `PrimeSpectrum` and `MaximalSpectrum` topologies serve.
- **General varieties and morphisms (§6.2–6.3)**: Propositions 1–4, the general notion of morphism, and Problems 6.10∗, 6.14∗–6.16∗, 6.19∗, 6.20∗ in classical form. The scheme versions in Mathlib (table) cover them should they ever be needed. Proposition 4 (Segre) is in neither library.
- **Products and graphs (§6.4)**: Propositions 6–8 and the Corollary to Proposition 7. They matter only if the resolution page builds §7.3's blow-up inside $\mathbb{P}^2 \times (\mathbb{P}^1)^t$; then use Mathlib's scheme products.
- **Problems 6.43∗ and 6.32∗**: projecting a space curve birationally onto a plane curve. Chapter 7 uses them only to show that the nonsingular model of a curve in $\mathbb{P}^n$, $n \geq 3$, maps onto it; a plane-curve library needs only plane curves, for which Fulton's own argument (via Problem 7.13) suffices.
- **Unstarred problems**: 6.5, 6.9, 6.11, 6.17, 6.18, 6.21, 6.27 (topology and examples of non-affine varieties; Tau Ceti constructs the punctured plane $\mathbb{A}^2 \setminus 0$ as a scheme, `TauCeti.puncturedAffineSpace` in `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/PuncturedAffineSpace.lean`, but we found no theorem there that it is not affine); 6.28 (universal family of plane curves of degree $d$); 6.29–6.30 (algebraic groups, the group laws on cuspidal and nodal cubics); 6.34–6.36, 6.40, 6.41 (dimension and hypersurface models in higher dimension; 6.36 for $r = 1$ is the plane-model milestone); 6.37–6.39 and 6.44 (rationality of conics and of singular cubics, $\mathbb{P}^n \times \mathbb{P}^m$ birational to $\mathbb{P}^{n+m}$, a rational space curve with no planar neighbourhood; the plane-curve examples are better treated on the Chapter 7–8 pages through the genus); 6.42 (isomorphic local rings give isomorphic neighbourhoods); 6.46 (a nonconstant function as a map to $\mathbb{P}^1$); 6.47 (the dual curve, which Appendix A uses in characteristic $p$ for "terrible" points — relevant to Chapter 7, Lemma 1 of §7.4, not to this page).
