# Resolution of singularities

This page covers Chapter 7 of Fulton's *Algebraic Curves*, "Resolution of Singularities" [@Fulton2008, Chapter 7], pp. 81–95 (p. 96 is blank): §7.1 Rational maps of curves (pp. 81–82), §7.2 Blowing up a point in $\mathbb{A}^2$ (pp. 82–85), §7.3 Blowing up points in $\mathbb{P}^2$ (pp. 86–87), §7.4 Quadratic transformations (pp. 87–92), §7.5 Nonsingular models of curves (pp. 92–95), and the remarks on Chapter 7 in Appendix A (p. 113).

The chapter does three things. It shows that a place (a discrete valuation ring of the function field) has a unique centre on a projective curve, so that the points of a nonsingular projective curve are the places of its function field (§7.1). It studies two ways of improving the singularities of a plane curve: blowing up a point, which keeps the curve's local rings under control (§§7.2–7.3), and the quadratic (Cremona) transformation, which keeps the curve in the plane and reduces every plane curve to one with only ordinary multiple points (§7.4). Finally it builds the nonsingular model of any projective curve, proves its uniqueness, and computes intersection numbers $I(P, C \cap G)$ as sums of orders at the places over $P$ (§7.5); this last formula, and the criterion for Noether's conditions at an ordinary multiple point, are what Chapter 8 (Riemann–Roch) uses.

## Conventions

### The book

- **The field.** $k$ is algebraically closed throughout (from Chapter 3 on). The characteristic is arbitrary except where noted:
  - Lemma 1 of §7.4 assumes $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ explicitly, and Appendix A (p. 113) says it is **false** in characteristic $p$ (at "terrible" points). Theorem 2 of §7.4 holds in every characteristic; Appendix A sketches the modification.
  - Theorem 3 of §7.5 is proved through the Corollary of §6.6 (every curve is birational to a plane curve), whose proof uses the primitive element theorem in characteristic 0 (Proposition 9 (2) of §6.5); Appendix A (p. 113) repairs this with a separating transcendental element, $k$ being perfect.
  - Problems 7.13 and 7.21 (a), (c), the proof of Lemma 2 of §7.5 (through Problem 7.13), and the surjectivity part of the proof of Theorem 3 (through Problems 7.13 and 6.43) rest on Lemma 1 or on Problem 6.32, so as written they assume characteristic 0. Problem 7.21 (b) (prescribed orders, through Problem 5.15) does not. The *statements* of Lemma 2 and Theorem 3 hold in every characteristic (by the normalization route recommended below). Whether the strong forms in Problems 7.13 and 7.21 (a), (c) hold in characteristic $p$ is not addressed by the book (see Notes on the source).
  - Everything else in the chapter (§§7.1–7.3, Steps (1)–(8) of §7.4, Propositions 2–3, Lemma 3) holds in every characteristic; the book's proof of Proposition 2 goes through Lemma 2, hence through Problem 7.13.
- **Curves.** In Chapters 6–7 a *curve* is a variety of dimension one, so irreducible; a *projective curve* is a projective variety of dimension one, not necessarily in the plane; a *plane curve* is a form (or polynomial) up to a nonzero scalar, possibly reducible and with multiple components; an *irreducible projective plane curve* is both. A point $P$ of an arbitrary curve $C$ is **simple** if $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ is a DVR (this agrees with the plane-curve definition by Theorem 1 of §3.2); $\operatorname{ord}^C_P$ is its order function; $C$ is nonsingular if all its points are simple (p. 81).
- **Local rings of a field.** A *local ring of* $K \supseteq k$ is a local subring $A \subseteq K$ with $k \subseteq A$ and fraction field $K$; a *DVR of* $K$ is a DVR that is a local ring of $K$ (p. 81). $B$ *dominates* $A$ if $A \subseteq B$ and $\mathfrak{m}_A \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_B$ (§6.6, p. 77).
- **Notation clashes.** $m_P(F)$ is the multiplicity of $F$ at $P$, $\mathfrak{m}_P(C)$ the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ (the book prints both with an "m"). $C'$ and $F'$ denote the strict transform under the blow-up in §§7.2–7.3 and the quadratic transform in §7.4: two different constructions with the same notation.
- **Infinity.** $\operatorname{ord}_P(0) = \infty$ and $I(P, F \cap G) = \infty$ when $F$, $G$ share a component through $P$.
- **Places.** In §7.5 the points of the nonsingular model $X$ of $C$ are called *places* of $C$ (or of $K = k(C)$), and a place $Q$ is *centred at* $P \in C$ if $f(Q) = P$, equivalently $\mathcal{O}_Q(X)$ dominates $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ (p. 92).
- **Coordinates in §7.4.** $[X : Y : Z]$; fundamental points $P = [0:0:1]$, $P' = [0:1:0]$, $P'' = [1:0:0]$; exceptional lines $L = V(Z)$, $L' = V(Y)$, $L'' = V(X)$; $Q[x:y:z] = [yz : xz : xy]$; $F^T = F \circ T$ for a projective change of coordinates $T$.

### The libraries, and the traps

- **Places in Tau Ceti.** `TauCeti.Place k F` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Place/Basic.lean) is a *normalized* valuation with values in `ℤᵐ⁰` (multiplicative $\mathbb{Z}$ with a zero adjoined), surjective and trivial on $k$; equality of places is equality of valuations. Mathlib's multiplicative convention is used: $\mathcal{O}_P = \{v \le 1\}$ and a uniformizer has $v(t) = \exp(-1)$. `TauCeti.Place.ord` is the additive order but has the **junk value** `ord P 0 = 0` where Fulton has $\infty$: a formula such as $I(P, C\cap G) = \sum_Q \operatorname{ord}_Q(G)$ is false with the junk value when $F \mid G$. Carry the hypothesis $g \neq 0$, or work in `ℕ∞`/`WithTop ℤ`.
- **Places versus DVRs.** Fulton's "DVR of $K$" and Tau Ceti's place are interchangeable: `TauCeti.Place.integers` is injective (`TauCeti.Place.integers_injective`), and a proper valuation subring containing $k$ is the ring of a unique place (`TauCeti.Place.ofValuationSubring`, `TauCeti.Place.existsUnique_integers_eq`, using `TauCeti.isDiscreteValuationRing_of_isFunctionField`). Stichtenoth's places [@Stichtenoth2009] (maximal ideals of valuation rings) are a third equivalent convention.
- **Degrees.** Tau Ceti's formulas carry the degree $\deg Q = [\mathcal{O}_Q/\mathfrak{m}_Q : k]$ of a place; over algebraically closed $k$ it is $1$ (`TauCeti.Place.degree_eq_one_iff_algebraMap_surjective`), and Fulton's formulas have no degree factors.
- **Function fields.** `TauCeti.IsFunctionField k F` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Basic.lean) is a `Prop` passed as an explicit hypothesis `hF`, not a class; it is equivalent to transcendence degree one for essentially finite type extensions (`TauCeti.isFunctionField_iff_trdeg_eq_one`). Mathlib's `FunctionField` class (a finite extension of a chosen `RatFunc`) is not the notion to use.
- **Domination in Mathlib.** `LocalSubring K` (Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean) is ordered by domination, with `A ≤ B` meaning that **B dominates A** (`LocalSubring.le_def`); maximal elements are exactly valuation subrings (`LocalSubring.isMax_iff`, Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean).
- **Curves as schemes.** Tau Ceti's curves are schemes: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IsCurveOver k` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/Basic.lean) is "integral, proper over $\operatorname{Spec} k$, of dimension one". Fulton's points are the closed points, which for algebraically closed $k$ are the $k$-points and the `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.CodimensionOnePoint`s; the generic point is extra. "Regular" (DVR local rings) equals "smooth" over a perfect field (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IsCurveOver.smoothOfRelativeDimension_one_of_perfectField`). `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve hG` is $\operatorname{Proj}(R[X_0,X_1,X_2]/(G))$ for any homogeneous $G$ (non-reduced allowed); it is integral for irreducible $G$ (instance `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve.isIntegral`), but Tau Ceti proves it is a curve (`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve.isCurveOver`, TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/PlaneCurve/Smooth.lean) only for nonsingular $G$.
- **Blow-ups.** Tau Ceti's blow-ups are scheme-theoretic ($\operatorname{Proj}$ of the Rees algebra; the exceptional divisor is the scheme-theoretic inverse image of the centre). Fulton's are explicit varieties: graph closures in $\mathbb{A}^3$ or $\mathbb{P}^2 \times (\mathbb{P}^1)^t$. Fulton's chart $\psi(x,z) = (x, xz)$ is the chart $\operatorname{Spec} A[I/x]$ for $I = (x,y)$ (`Ideal.affineBlowup`, TauCeti/RingTheory/Ideal/AffineBlowup.lean).
- **Lengths.** Mathlib's `Ring.ord` (Mathlib/RingTheory/OrderOfVanishing/Basic.lean) is `Module.length R (R ⧸ (x))` in `ℕ∞`; $\dim_k$ is `Module.finrank` in `ℕ` (junk $0$ for infinite dimension). They agree on the local rings here (residue field $k$) but have different types.
- **Indexing.** Mathlib's `MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k` has variables $X_0, X_1, X_2$; decide once which one is Fulton's $Z$ (the fundamental point $P = [0:0:1]$ and the line $L = V(Z)$ depend on it).

### Recommended representation (advice)

- **Plane-curve computations at polynomial level.** The blow-up equation $F' = X^{-r}F(X, XZ)$ (§7.2), the algebraic and proper transforms $F^Q = F(YZ, XZ, XY)$ and $F'$ (§7.4), multiplicities, tangents and intersection numbers are polynomial algebra and should use the representation of plane curves fixed on the Chapter 3 and Chapter 5 pages (polynomials or forms up to units). Substitution is `MvPolynomial.aeval`/`MvPolynomial.bind₁`; `MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.aeval` gives homogeneity of $F^Q$.
- **The nonsingular model through places.** For an irreducible plane curve $C = V(F)$ let $K$ be its function field (the fraction field of an affine coordinate ring $k[x,y]$, a `TauCeti.IsFunctionField` by `TauCeti.isFunctionField_of_aeval_eq_zero`, or `(projectivePlaneCurve hG).functionField` through `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve.functionFieldEquiv`, TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/PlaneCurve/Basic.lean). Take the **set of places** `TauCeti.Place k K` as the points of the nonsingular model, and define the **centre map** $\mathrm{Place}\ k\ K \to C$ by domination (milestone "Centre of a discrete valuation ring on a projective curve"). Then the places centred at $P$ are $\{Q \mid \mathcal{O}_P(C) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_Q\}$, $\operatorname{ord}_Q$ is `TauCeti.Place.ord`, and Chapter 8 (divisors, $L(D)$, genus, Riemann–Roch) can reuse Tau Ceti's function-field theory over `Place k K` directly (`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Divisor/`, `RiemannRoch/`).
- **The nonsingular model as a curve, when needed.** Do not rebuild Fulton's projective variety (Proposition 1 of §7.3 with the Segre embedding, Theorem 2). Tau Ceti already has the regular projective curve of a function field, `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.twoChartModel`, with its closed points identified with places (`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.twoChartModel.equivPlace`), the anti-equivalence with function fields, and the normalization of an integral curve; see the milestones of §7.5. Fulton's blow-ups and quadratic transformations remain milestones because Chapter 7's local statements (Steps (2)–(3) of §7.2) feed Lemma 3 and Proposition 3, and because Theorem 2 is a theorem about plane curves in its own right.

## Milestones

### §7.1 Rational maps of curves

> [!milestone] Centre of a discrete valuation ring on a projective curve
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C$ a projective curve (irreducible, of dimension one, possibly singular) with function field $K = k(C)$, $L \supseteq K$ a field, and $R$ a DVR of $L$ (so $k \subseteq R$ and $L$ is the fraction field of $R$) with $K \not\subseteq R$. Then there is exactly one point $P \in C$ such that $R$ dominates $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$, i.e. $\mathcal{O}_P(C) \subseteq R$ and $\mathfrak{m}_P(C) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_R$. Under these hypotheses domination is equivalent to the inclusion $\mathcal{O}_P(C) \subseteq R$ (if $\mathfrak{m}_R \cap \mathcal{O}_P(C) = 0$, every nonzero element of $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ is a unit of $R$ and $K \subseteq R$). If $K \subseteq R$, no point is dominated. The case $L = K$: every place of $K$ has exactly one centre on $C$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.1, Theorem 1] (p. 81).
>
> Proof routes: Uniqueness: for $P \neq Q$ there is $f \in \mathfrak{m}_P(C)$ with $1/f \in \mathcal{O}_Q(C)$ (Problem 6.24), which cannot have $\operatorname{ord}_R(f) > 0$ and $\operatorname{ord}_R(1/f) \geq 0$. Existence: embed $C \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$ meeting every $U_i$ (Problem 6.25); among the ratios $x_i/x_j$ of homogeneous coordinates pick one of maximal order $N$, say $\operatorname{ord}(x_j/x_{n+1}) = N$; then $\operatorname{ord}(x_i/x_{n+1}) = N - \operatorname{ord}(x_j/x_i) \geq 0$ for all $i$, so $R$ contains the affine coordinate ring $\Gamma(C_*)$ of $C \cap U_{n+1}$; $J = \mathfrak{m}_R \cap \Gamma(C_*)$ is a nonzero prime (else $K \subseteq R$), hence the ideal of a point $P$ (Proposition 10 (4) of §6.5, Problem 2.2), and $R$ dominates $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$. Alternative: the valuative criterion of properness (existence) and of separatedness (uniqueness) for $C \to \operatorname{Spec} k$.
>
> Formalization notes: For the plane-curve library, state it for $C = V(F) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2$, $F$ irreducible: the proof is valuation arithmetic on three coordinates and needs no schemes. The residue field of $R$ may be larger than $k$ when $L$ is not a function field over $k$, so define the centre through the maximal ideal $J$, not through residues of coordinates; in the only case used later ($L$ a function field over $k$, places of degree one) the centre is $[\bar x_0 : \bar x_1 : \bar x_2]$ with $\bar x_j$ the residues of $x_j/x_i$. With $Q$ : `TauCeti.Place k L` and $L/K$ finite, the hypothesis $K \not\subseteq \mathcal{O}_Q$ is automatic (Corollary 1's argument), and the centre of $Q$ is the centre of `TauCeti.Place.restrict` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Place/Extension/Basic.lean). The book's proof uses the starred Problems 6.24, 6.25 and 2.2 (see [varieties, morphisms and rational maps](../auxiliary/varieties-and-rational-maps.md) and [affine varieties](../auxiliary/affine-varieties.md)). This milestone defines the centre map of the recommended representation.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `AlgebraicGeometry.IsProper.eq_valuativeCriterion`, `AlgebraicGeometry.IsSeparated.valuativeCriterion` (Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ValuativeCriterion.lean) are the valuative criteria for schemes; `LocalSubring` and its domination order (Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/LocalSubring.lean). Tau Ceti **partial**: `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.CodimensionOnePoint.exists_toPlace_eq_of_lift` and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.CodimensionOnePoint.toPlace_surjective` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/Place/Proper.lean) and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.CodimensionOnePoint.toPlace_injective` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/Place/Injective.lean) prove existence and uniqueness of the centre of a place of $k(X)$ on an integral scheme $X$ of dimension $\leq 1$ satisfying the valuative criterion, but only under the standing hypothesis that every codimension-one local ring is a DVR (nonsingular curves) and only for $L = k(X)$; the singular case and $L \supsetneq K$ are not stated.

> [!milestone] Rational maps from a curve to a projective curve are defined at simple points
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C'$ a curve and $C$ a projective curve. (a) Every rational map $f : C' \dashrightarrow C$ is defined at every simple point of $C'$; if $C'$ is nonsingular, $f$ is a morphism. (b) If $C'$ is nonsingular (not necessarily projective), $f \mapsto \tilde f$ is a bijection between dominant morphisms $C' \to C$ and $k$-algebra homomorphisms $k(C) \to k(C')$. (c) Two nonsingular projective curves are isomorphic if and only if their function fields are $k$-isomorphic.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.1, Corollaries 1–3] (p. 82).
>
> Proof routes: (a) A non-dominating rational map between curves is constant (Problem 6.45), hence defined everywhere. Otherwise $\tilde f$ embeds $K = k(C)$ in $L = k(C')$ with $L/K$ finite (Problem 6.45); for a simple point $P$, $R = \mathcal{O}_P(C')$ does not contain $K$, since otherwise $R$ would be a field (Problem 1.50); Theorem 1 gives the point of $C$ dominated by $R$, and Proposition 11 (2) of §6.6 says $f$ is defined at $P$. (b), (c) follow with Proposition 11 (3) of §6.6. Alternative: the valuative criterion (Tau Ceti's route below).
>
> Formalization notes: "Dominant" for a map into a curve means non-constant (Problem 6.45). In the recommended representation, (a) for nonsingular $C'$ is the statement that each place of $L$ has a centre on $C$ (previous milestone composed with `TauCeti.Place.restrict`); the morphism structure, if wanted, comes from the scheme statements below. Uses the starred Problems 6.45 and 1.50.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap.equivFunctionFieldOver` (Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/RationalMap.lean). Tau Ceti **full** for nonsingular sources, (b) and (c), in scheme form, and **partial** for (a) at simple points of a singular source: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.RationalMap.domain_eq_top_of_valuationRing` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.homOfFunctionField` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/RationalMap.lean) extend rational maps from integral schemes all of whose local rings are valuation rings into universally closed (proper) targets; `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom`, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.functionFieldAlgHom_homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom`, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom_functionFieldAlgHom` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.isoOfFunctionFieldAlgEquiv` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Birational/AlgHom.lean) give (b) and (c). The pointwise statement at one simple point of a singular curve is not stated.

> [!milestone] Points of a nonsingular projective curve are the places of its function field
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C$ a nonsingular projective curve and $K = k(C)$. Then $P \mapsto \mathcal{O}_P(C)$ is a bijection from $C$ onto the set of DVRs of $K$ (containing $k$, with fraction field $K$). Giving that set the topology whose nonempty open sets are the cofinite ones, the bijection is a homeomorphism, and for $U \subseteq C$ open, $\Gamma(U, \mathcal{O}_C) = \bigcap_{P \in U} \mathcal{O}_P(C)$; so $C$ is determined up to isomorphism by $K$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.1, Corollary 4 and the paragraph after it] (p. 82).
>
> Proof routes: Each $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ is a DVR of $K$. Given a DVR $R$ of $K$, Theorem 1 gives $P$ with $R$ dominating $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$; two DVRs of $K$ one dominating the other are equal (Problem 2.26 (b)). Injectivity is Problem 6.24.
>
> Formalization notes: In the recommended representation this is the definition of the points of the nonsingular model as `TauCeti.Place k K`; the translation between "DVR of $K$" and `Place` is `TauCeti.Place.ofValuationSubring` with `TauCeti.isDiscreteValuationRing_of_isFunctionField` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/Place/OfValuationSubring.lean). Problem 2.26 (b) (a DVR is maximal for domination) is Mathlib's `ValuationSubring.isMax_toLocalSubring` and Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Place.eq_of_integers_le`. The ring $\bigcap_{P\in U}\mathcal{O}_P$ is `TauCeti.holomorphyRing` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/HolomorphyRing/Basic.lean). The cofinite topology is not used in later chapters; it can be omitted unless a variety structure on the set of places is wanted. The classical construction of the abstract nonsingular curve from the DVRs of $K$ is also in [@Hartshorne1977]. Uses the starred Problems 2.26 and 6.24.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `IsDiscreteValuationRing` (Mathlib/RingTheory/DiscreteValuationRing/Basic.lean), `ValuationSubring`, `ValuationSubring.isMax_toLocalSubring` (Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean). Tau Ceti **full** in scheme form: `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.CodimensionOnePoint.equivPlace` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/Place/Proper.lean) is a bijection between the codimension-one points of an integral separated $k$-scheme of dimension $\leq 1$ with DVR local rings satisfying the valuative existence criterion (a nonsingular projective curve) and `TauCeti.Place k X.functionField`; `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.toPlace` and `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.stalkToPlaceIntegersAlgEquiv` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme/Place/Basic.lean) identify the local ring with the valuation ring of the place; `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.twoChartModel.equivPlace` (TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/TwoChartModel/Proper.lean) is the same for the model of a function field. ### §7.2 Blowing up a point in $\mathbb{A}^2$

> [!milestone] Blowing up a plane curve at a point: strict transform, fibre and local finiteness
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $F \in k[X,Y]$ irreducible, $C = V(F) \neq V(X)$, $P = (0,0) \in C$, $r = m_P(F) \geq 1$, $n = \deg F$, $F = F_r + F_{r+1} + \dots + F_n$ with $F_i$ forms of degree $i$. Let $\psi : \mathbb{A}^2 \to \mathbb{A}^2$, $\psi(x,z) = (x, xz)$, and $E = V(X)$, so $\psi$ is an isomorphism from $\mathbb{A}^2 \setminus E$ onto $\{x \neq 0\}$.
> 1. $F(X, XZ) = X^r F'$ with $F' = F_r(1,Z) + X F_{r+1}(1,Z) + \dots + X^{n-r} F_n(1,Z)$; $F'$ is irreducible and not divisible by $X$, $C' = V(F')$ is the closure of $\psi^{-1}(C \setminus V(X))$, and $\psi$ restricts to a birational morphism $f : C' \to C$, an isomorphism over $C \setminus V(X)$, identifying $k(C) = k(x,y)$ with $k(C') = k(x,z)$, $y = xz$.
> 2. Suppose $X$ is not tangent to $C$ at $P$, and scale $F$ so that $F_r = \prod_{i=1}^s (Y - \alpha_i X)^{r_i}$ with $\alpha_i$ distinct, $\sum r_i = r$. Then $f^{-1}(P) = \{P_1, \dots, P_s\}$ with $P_i = (0, \alpha_i)$, and $m_{P_i}(C') \leq I(P_i, C' \cap E) = r_i$. If $P$ is an ordinary multiple point ($s = r$), each $P_i$ is a simple point of $C'$ and $\operatorname{ord}^{C'}_{P_i}(x) = 1$.
> 3. Under the same assumption there is an affine open neighbourhood $W$ of $P$ in $C$ such that $W' = f^{-1}(W)$ is affine, $f(W') = W$, $\Gamma(W') = \Gamma(W)[z]$ is module-finite over $\Gamma(W)$, and $x^{r-1}\Gamma(W') \subseteq \Gamma(W)$. $W$ and $W'$ may be chosen inside any given neighbourhoods of $P$ and of $f^{-1}(P)$ and, after a linear change of coordinates, containing any given finite set of points of $C$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.2, Steps (1)–(3), the Assumption and the Remarks] (pp. 83–85).
>
> Proof routes: (1) Substitute and factor out $X^r$; irreducibility because a factorization $F' = GH$ would give $F = X^r G(X, Y/X) H(X, Y/X)$. (2) $F'(0, Z) = F_r(1, Z) = \prod (Z - \alpha_i)^{r_i}$, so $I(P_i, F' \cap X) = r_i$, and $m_{P_i} \leq I$ by the properties of intersection numbers. (3) With $F = \sum a_{ij} X^i Y^j$ and $H = \sum_j a_{0j} Y^{j-r}$ ($H(0,0) = 1$ by the scaling), $W = C_h$; the identity $F'(x,z) = \sum a_{ij} y^{i+j-r} z^{r-i}$ gives a monic equation of degree $r$ for $z$ over $\Gamma(W)$, whence finiteness (Proposition 3 of §1.9) and $x^{r-1} z^i \in \Gamma(W)$ for $i < r$.
>
> Formalization notes: Do (1)–(2) at polynomial level: $F' $ is `MvPolynomial.aeval ![X 0, X 0 * X 1] F` divided by `X 0 ^ r`. The natural generality is any $F$ (then $F'$ is multiplicative in $F$; Problem 7.3 asks for $F$ without multiple components), with irreducibility of $F'$ only for irreducible $F$; the Assumption can be removed by also using the second chart $(w, y) \mapsto (wy, y)$, or arranged by the change of coordinates $(X,Y) \mapsto (X + \alpha Y, Y)$. > [!milestone] Orders at the places over an ordinary multiple point
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C = V(F)$ an irreducible plane curve, $P$ an ordinary multiple point of multiplicity $r$ with tangents $L_1, \dots, L_r$. Then exactly $r$ places $Q_1, \dots, Q_r$ of $K = k(C)$ are centred at $P$, $Q_i$ corresponding to $L_i$ (in the coordinates of the previous milestone, $L_i = Y - \alpha_i X$ and $Q_i$ is the place of the simple point $P_i = (0, \alpha_i)$ of $C'$), and a linear form through $P$ not tangent to $C$ has order $1$ at every $Q_i$. For every plane curve $G$ not containing $C$ as a component:
> 1. $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(G) \geq m_P(G)$, with equality if $L_i$ is not tangent to $G$ at $P$;
> 2. for $s \leq r$: $m_P(G) \geq s$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(G) \geq s$ for all $i$;
> 3. if $X$ is not tangent to $C$ at $P = (0,0)$, then $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(F_Y) = r - 1$ for all $i$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.2, Problems 7.4, 7.5] (p. 85); [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Problem 7.19] (p. 94). All three problems are starred.
>
> Proof routes: (1) $g = G(x, xz) = x^{m}\,(G_m(1,z) + x(\dots))$ with $m = m_P(G)$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(x) = 1$; $G_m(1,z)$ vanishes at $z = \alpha_i$ exactly when $L_i$ is tangent to $G$. (2) If $m_P(G) = m < s \leq r$, $G$ has at most $m < r$ tangents at $P$, so some $L_i$ is not tangent to $G$, and $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(G) = m < s$ by (1); the converse is (1). (3) $F_Y = \sum_i \prod_{j \neq i}(Y - \alpha_j X) + (F_{r+1})_Y + \dots$, so $F_Y(x, xz) = x^{r-1}\bigl(\sum_i \prod_{j\neq i}(z - \alpha_j) + x(\dots)\bigr)$, and at $z = \alpha_i$ the bracket is $\prod_{j \neq i}(\alpha_i - \alpha_j) \neq 0$.
>
> Formalization notes: All three are starred problems used in Chapter 8: (2) (as Problem 7.19) characterizes adjoints (§8.1), (3) (Problem 7.4) is used for canonical divisors (§8.5). In the place representation, $Q_i$ ranges over the places centred at $P$, and $\operatorname{ord}_Q(G)$ is `TauCeti.Place.ord` of the image of $G_*$ (the trap: Tau Ceti's value at $0$ is $0$, so keep "$C \not\subseteq G$"). (3) holds in every characteristic. The count "exactly $r$ places" also follows from Proposition 2 of §7.5 applied to a non-tangent line.
>

### §7.3 Blowing up points in $\mathbb{P}^2$

> [!milestone] Resolution of a plane curve with only ordinary multiple points by blowing up
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $C$ an irreducible projective plane curve all of whose multiple points $P_1, \dots, P_t$ are ordinary. Let $B \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2 \times (\mathbb{P}^1)^t$ be the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at $P_1, \dots, P_t$ (the closure of the graph of the $t$ projections from the $P_i$), $\pi : B \to \mathbb{P}^2$ the projection, and $C'$ the closure in $B$ of $\pi^{-1}(C \setminus \{P_1, \dots, P_t\})$. Then $C'$ is a nonsingular projective curve and $\pi$ restricts to a birational morphism $f : C' \to C$ onto $C$, an isomorphism over $C \setminus \{P_i\}$, with $f^{-1}(P_i)$ in bijection with the $m_{P_i}(C)$ tangents at $P_i$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.3, Proposition 1 and Steps (1)–(8)] (pp. 86–87).
>
> Proof routes: Book: Steps (1)–(7) show that $B$ is a variety (the closure of the graph, Step (6)), that $\pi$ is an isomorphism off $\bigcup E_i$, $E_i = \pi^{-1}(P_i) \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, and that near any point of $E_i$ the map $\pi$ is, in suitable coordinates (Steps (3)–(4), Problems 7.7, 7.8), the affine blow-up $\psi$ of §7.2; Step (8) and Step (2) of §7.2 then give nonsingularity. Alternative (recommended): Proposition 1 is the special case of the nonsingular model (Theorem 3) for curves with ordinary multiple points, together with the milestone "Orders at the places over an ordinary multiple point", which gives the fibres.
>
> Formalization notes: Fulton's construction needs multiprojective varieties and the Segre embedding to know that $C'$ is projective (Proposition 4 of §6.3; see [projective varieties](../auxiliary/projective-varieties.md)). If the library wants the blow-up as an object, Tau Ceti's scheme blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ along the reduced points and its strict transform (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.ker_blowupBaseChangeHom`) are the tools, and the local computation is §7.2. The starred Problems 7.7 and 7.8 (compatibility of the projections with projective changes of coordinates) are needed only for Fulton's construction.
>

### §7.4 Quadratic transformations

> [!milestone] Proper transform of a plane curve under the standard quadratic transformation
> Let $k$ be a field (algebraically closed in the book; (1)–(3) are polynomial identities valid over any field) and $Q : \mathbb{P}^2 \dashrightarrow \mathbb{P}^2$, $Q[x:y:z] = [yz : xz : xy]$, defined off the fundamental points; $Q$ maps $U = \mathbb{P}^2 \setminus V(XYZ)$ isomorphically onto itself with $Q \circ Q = \mathrm{id}_U$, and $Q^{-1}(P) = L \setminus \{P', P''\}$. Let $F$ be an irreducible form of degree $n$ that is not an exceptional line, $C = V(F)$, $r = m_P(F)$, $r' = m_{P'}(F)$, $r'' = m_{P''}(F)$, and let $F^Q = F(YZ, XZ, XY)$ (the algebraic transform, a form of degree $2n$). Then:
> 1. $Z^r$ is the largest power of $Z$ dividing $F^Q$ (similarly $Y^{r'}$, $X^{r''}$), so $F^Q = X^{r''} Y^{r'} Z^{r} F'$ with $X, Y, Z \nmid F'$ (the proper transform);
> 2. $\deg F' = 2n - r - r' - r''$, $F'$ is irreducible, $(F')' = F$, and $V(F') = C'$, the closure of $Q^{-1}(C \cap U)$; $Q$ restricts to a birational morphism $C' \setminus \{P,P',P''\} \to C$;
> 3. $m_P(F') = n - r' - r''$, $m_{P'}(F') = n - r - r''$, $m_{P''}(F') = n - r - r'$;
> 4. if $C$ is in good position (no exceptional line is tangent to $C$ at a fundamental point), so is $C'$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Steps (1)–(4) and the definitions before them] (pp. 87–89).
>
> Proof routes: Write $F = \sum_{i \geq 0} F_{r+i}(X,Y) Z^{n-r-i}$ (Problem 5.5) and substitute: $F^Q = Z^r\bigl(F_r(Y,X)(XY)^{n-r} + Z F_{r+1}(Y,X)(XY)^{n-r-1} + \dots\bigr)$. (2) from $(F^Q)^Q = (XYZ)^n F$. (3) the lowest-degree form of $F'$ at $[0:0:1]$ is $F_n(Y,X)\,X^{-r''} Y^{-r'}$. (4) $L$ is tangent to $C'$ at $P'$ iff $I(P', F' \cap Z) > m_{P'}(F')$ iff $F_r(1,0) = 0$ iff $Y$ is tangent to $C$ at $P$.
>
> Formalization notes: Pure polynomial algebra: $F^Q$ = `MvPolynomial.bind₁ ![X 1 * X 2, X 0 * X 2, X 0 * X 1] F` (with $X_0, X_1, X_2 = X, Y, Z$), homogeneous of degree $2n$ by `MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.aeval`; prove $(F^Q)^Q = (XYZ)^n F$ first. The construction is multiplicative ($(GH)' = G'H'$), so (1)–(3) hold for any form with no exceptional line as a component, which is the generality Problem 7.16 (a) needs; irreducibility transfers. Multiplicities at points of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and tangency come from the [Chapter 5 page](02-projective-plane-curves.md). Uses the starred Problem 5.5.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib —; related API: `MvPolynomial.bind₁` (Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Monad.lean), `MvPolynomial.IsHomogeneous.aeval` (Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Homogeneous.lean). Tau Ceti —.

> [!milestone] Quadratic transformation centred at a point and its new singularities
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $F$ an irreducible projective plane curve of degree $n$, $C = V(F)$.
> 1. (Existence; $\operatorname{char} k = 0$.) For every $P \in F$ (the book's Lemma 1; the same proof works for any $P \in \mathbb{P}^2$, $r = m_P(F) \geq 0$, as Problem 7.14 needs) there is a projective change of coordinates $T$ with $T([0:0:1]) = P$ and $F^T$ in **excellent position**: good position, $L$ meets $F^T$ transversally in $n$ distinct non-fundamental points, and $L'$, $L''$ each meet $F^T$ transversally in $n - r$ distinct non-fundamental points besides $[0:0:1]$. Then $(F^T)'$ is a *quadratic transformation of $F$ centred at $P$*. In characteristic $p$ this can fail (Appendix A).
> 2. (Singularities; any characteristic.) If $C$ is in excellent position and $r = m_P(C)$, then $C'$ has degree $2n - r$ and its multiple points are: (a) the points of $C' \cap U$ corresponding to the multiple points of $C \cap U$ (all multiple points of $C$ other than $P$), with the same multiplicities, ordinary ones corresponding to ordinary ones; (b) $P$, $P'$, $P''$, ordinary of multiplicities $n$, $n-r$, $n-r$ (when $n - r \leq 1$, $P'$ and $P''$ are simple points of $C'$ or not on $C'$, and when $n = 1$, $P$ is a simple point; the book still calls them ordinary multiple points); (c) non-fundamental points $P_1, \dots, P_s$ of $C' \cap L$, with $m_{P_i}(C') \leq I(P_i, C' \cap L)$ and $\sum_i I(P_i, C' \cap L) = r$; there are none on $L'$ or $L''$. Near $[0:0:1]$ the map is the affine blow-up of §7.2 (explicit open isomorphisms, Step (5)).
> 3. ($g^*$.) With $g^*(C) = \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} - \sum_Q \frac{m_Q(C)(m_Q(C)-1)}{2}$, $g^*(C') = g^*(C) - \sum_{i=1}^s \frac{r_i(r_i - 1)}{2}$, $r_i = m_{P_i}(C')$.
> 4. A quadratic transformation centred at an ordinary $r$-fold point, at a simple point, or at a point not on $C$ gives degree $2n - r$, $2n - 1$, $2n$ respectively and leaves $g^*$ unchanged.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Steps (5)–(8), Lemma 1, the definitions of good and excellent position and of a quadratic transformation centred at $P$] (pp. 88–90); [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Problem 7.14 (a)] (p. 91, starred); [@Fulton2008, Appendix A] (p. 113).
>
> Proof routes: (1) By Problem 5.26 (characteristic 0), all but finitely many lines through $P$ meet $F$ in $n - r$ distinct points besides $P$; choose $L'$, $L''$ this way and then $L$, all intersection points distinct, and move them to the coordinate lines (Problem 4.16). (2) Step (5): $\varphi(x,y) = [x:y:1]$, $\varphi'(x,z) = [z:1:xz]$ identify a neighbourhood of $P$ and its preimage with the affine blow-up. (a) because $C \cap U \cong C' \cap U$ and multiplicity and ordinariness are properties of the local ring (Theorem 2 of §3.2, Problem 3.24); (c) from Step (6), i.e. $\sum I(P_i, F' \cap Z) = \sum I(P_i, F_r(Y,X) \cap Z) = r$; (b) from Step (6) applied to $C'$ and $(C')' = C$, using Step (4). (3) arithmetic from (2). (4) from (3): at an ordinary point the $r$ points $P_i$ are distinct with $I = 1$.
>
> Formalization notes: Excellent position is not symmetric in the three fundamental points. The transfer in (2)(a) needs the intrinsic characterizations of multiplicity and ordinariness by the local ring from the [Chapter 3 page](01-local-properties.md) (Theorem 2 of §3.2 and the starred Problem 3.24); there is no linear change of coordinates relating $C$ and $C'$ near those points. $g^*(C) \geq 0$ is Theorem 2 of §5.4 ([Chapter 5 page](02-projective-plane-curves.md)). For a characteristic-free development, state (1) under the hypothesis "characteristic 0 or $P$ not terrible for $F$" (Appendix A: $P$ is terrible if infinitely many lines through $P$ meet $F$ in fewer than $n - r$ further distinct points; this forces $p \mid n - r$, and at most one point is terrible). Uses the starred Problems 4.16, 5.26, 3.24.
>

> [!milestone] Reduction of a plane curve to ordinary multiple points by quadratic transformations
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, of any characteristic. Every irreducible projective plane curve $F$ can be transformed by a finite sequence of quadratic transformations $F = F_1, F_2, \dots, F_m$ into a curve $F_m$ all of whose multiple points are ordinary. In characteristic 0, $N + g^*(F)$ steps suffice, $N$ the number of non-ordinary multiple points of $F$. Strong form ($\operatorname{char} k = 0$ as written): (a) given a finite set $S$ of simple points of $F$, the sequence can be chosen so that a neighbourhood of $S$ in $F$ is isomorphic to an open subset of $F_m$ consisting of simple points; (b) $F_m$ can be taken of arbitrarily large degree; (c) consequently, for a nonsingular projective curve $X$ and points $P_1, \dots, P_s \in X$ there is a birational morphism $f : X \to C$ onto a projective plane curve $C$ with only ordinary multiple points, of arbitrarily large degree, with every $f(P_i)$ simple on $C$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Theorem 2] (p. 91); [@Fulton2008, Appendix A] (p. 113); [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Problems 7.13, 7.14 (b)] (p. 91); [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Problem 7.21 (a), (c)] (p. 94). Problems 7.13, 7.14 and 7.21 are starred.
>
> Proof routes: Centre each step at a non-ordinary multiple point. By the previous milestone, the old multiple points keep their type, the fundamental points are ordinary, and the new points $P_i$ on $L$ satisfy $g^*(C') = g^*(C) - \sum r_i(r_i-1)/2$; if no $P_i$ is multiple, $N$ drops by one, and each new non-ordinary point lowers $g^*$ by at least one, so $N + g^*$ strictly decreases. Characteristic $p$ (Appendix A): if $P$ is terrible (so $p \mid n - r$), first apply a quadratic transformation centred at a point $Q$ of multiplicity $m \in \{0, 1\}$, with $P$ off the fundamental lines, choosing $m$ with $p \nmid (2n - m) - r$ (and $Q$ itself not terrible, which the book leaves implicit; at most one point is terrible); this leaves $N$ and $g^*$ unchanged (Problem 7.14). (a) choose each $T$ with $T^{-1}(S) \cap V(XYZ) = \emptyset$ (Problem 7.13 (a)); (b) interleave transformations centred at simple points or points off the curve (Problem 7.14); (c) apply (a) to a plane model of $X$ and use (b).
>
> Formalization notes: The termination measure $N + g^*$ is a natural number by Theorem 2 of §5.4. If the library builds the nonsingular model through places (recommended), Theorem 2 is **not** needed for Theorem 3; it is needed if Chapter 8 is formalized along Fulton's route (adjoints of plane models with ordinary multiple points), which uses the strong forms (Problem 8.26 in §8.5, Problem 7.21 (a) in §8.6). Weak approximation, Problem 7.21 (b), used in §8.2, is already in Tau Ceti (see the table). The argument extends to curves without multiple components (Problem 7.16 (a)). The characteristic-$p$ argument is only sketched in the book, and the strong forms in characteristic $p$ are not addressed (see Notes on the source).
>

> [!milestone] Noether's formula for intersection numbers at infinitely near points
> Book's form: let $k$ be algebraically closed and $F$, $G$ projective plane curves without common components and without multiple components. Apply quadratic transformations to $FG$ (Problem 7.16 (a)) to obtain $F = F_1, \dots, F_s$ and $G = G_1, \dots, G_s$ whose last terms have only ordinary multiple points and no common tangents at common points, and let $P_{ij}$ be the "neighbouring singularities", the points introduced on the line $L$ at the $i$-th step. Then
> $$\deg F \cdot \deg G = \sum_{P} m_P(F)\, m_P(G) + \sum_{i > 1,\, j} m_{P_{ij}}(F_i)\, m_{P_{ij}}(G_i),$$
> and, for one irreducible $F$ of degree $n$ transformed to ordinary multiple points, $(n-1)(n-2) \geq \sum_P m_P(F)(m_P(F)-1) + \sum_{i>1,j} m_{P_{ij}}(F_i)(m_{P_{ij}}(F_i)-1)$.
> Local form (the natural general statement, not in the book): let $k$ be algebraically closed (any characteristic), $F, G \in k[X,Y]$ nonzero with no common component through $P = (0,0)$ (both may be reducible, with multiple components, and need not pass through $P$), $r = m_P(F)$, $s = m_P(G)$, $X$ not tangent to $F$ at $P$ (no hypothesis on $G$), $F' = X^{-r}F(X, XZ)$, $G' = X^{-s}G(X, XZ)$ (polynomials, since $X^r$, resp. $X^s$, is the exact power of $X$ dividing $F(X,XZ)$, resp. $G(X,XZ)$). Then
> $$I(P, F \cap G) = r s + \sum_{\alpha \in k} I\bigl((0,\alpha), F' \cap G'\bigr),$$
> a finite sum (only the roots $\alpha$ of $F_r(1, Z)$ contribute); iterating, $I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{Q} m_Q(F)\, m_Q(G)$ over the points $Q$ infinitely near $P$. Without the tangency hypothesis the formula needs the second chart of the blow-up as well (the point of the exceptional line in the direction of $X = 0$).
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.4, Problems 7.15, 7.16] (pp. 91–92). Infinitely near points are the subject of [@CasasAlvero2000, Chapter 3]; this check did not locate the local form there, so no location is claimed for it.
>
> Proof routes: Book's form: induction along the sequence using the degree and multiplicity formulas of the two previous milestones and Bézout's theorem for the final pair (at whose common points $I = m\,m$); the inequality is the same computation with $g^* \geq 0$. Local form via Proposition 2 of §7.5 (checked in this fact-check): both sides are additive in $F$ (because $(F_1F_2)' = F_1'F_2'$ and $m_P$ is additive), and a factor not through $P$ contributes $0$ to both sides, so let $F$ be irreducible through $P$, $C = V(F)$. Then $I(P, F \cap G) = \sum_{Q \text{ over } P} \operatorname{ord}_Q(g)$, $g = x^s g'$ with $g'$ the image of $G'$ in $k(C) = k(x, z)$, and $\sum_Q \operatorname{ord}_Q(x) = I(P, F \cap X) = r$ because $X$ is not tangent. Every place $Q$ over $P$ has its centre on the affine chart $C' = V(F')$, because $z = y/x$ is integral over $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ (the monic equation of Step (3) of §7.2, which uses the Assumption), so $\operatorname{ord}_Q(z) \geq 0$ and the centre is a point $(0, \alpha)$ of $f^{-1}(P)$; hence the places over $P$ are the places of $F'$ over the points $(0,\alpha)$, and Proposition 2 for $F'$ gives $\sum_Q \operatorname{ord}_Q(g') = \sum_\alpha I((0,\alpha), F' \cap G')$. The iteration (after a linear change of coordinates at each new point, so that the exceptional line is not tangent to the strict transform of $F$) terminates because $I$ strictly drops while both strict transforms pass through a common point ($rs \geq 1$ then).
>
> Formalization notes: This is the "formula for $I$ under blowing up"; prove the local form once and derive the book's global form with Bézout if wanted. Problems 7.15 and 7.16 are not starred and not used later in the book; the local form is the standard tool for the δ-invariant and genus formulas of later chapters (not in the book). Depends on "Intersection numbers as sums of orders at places" and the blow-up milestone of §7.2.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib —. Tau Ceti —; related: `TauCeti.length_quotient_span_pair_mul_eq_add_of_prime` (TauCeti/RingTheory/Intersection.lean), additivity of the local intersection length in one equation.

### §7.5 Nonsingular models of curves

> [!milestone] Existence and uniqueness of the nonsingular model of a projective curve
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $C$ a projective curve (any characteristic). There is a nonsingular projective curve $X$ with a birational morphism $f : X \to C$ that is surjective; for any other such $f' : X' \to C$ there is a unique isomorphism $g : X \to X'$ with $f' \circ g = f$. Identifying $k(X)$ with $K = k(C)$ by $\tilde f$, the points $Q$ of $X$ ("places of $C$") correspond to the DVRs $\mathcal{O}_Q(X)$ of $K$, and $f(Q) = P$ ("$Q$ is centred at $P$") exactly when $\mathcal{O}_Q(X)$ dominates $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$. Every $P \in C$ has a nonempty finite set of places centred at it, and exactly one, with $\mathcal{O}_Q(X) = \mathcal{O}_P(C)$, when $P$ is simple; for a plane curve their number is at most $m_P(C)$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Theorem 3 and the paragraph defining places] (p. 92); the clause on simple points is [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Problem 7.17 (b)] (p. 94). Finiteness of the fibres is used but not stated in the book, and the bound by $m_P(C)$ is not in the book; it follows from Proposition 2 of §7.5 with a line through $P$ not tangent to $C$.
>
> Proof routes: Book: uniqueness from Corollary 2 of §7.1; existence: $C$ is birational to a plane curve (Corollary of §6.6), which by Theorem 2 may be taken with ordinary multiple points, and Proposition 1 of §7.3 resolves it; Corollary 1 of §7.1 makes $X \to C$ a morphism; surjectivity by Problem 7.13 (plane case) and Problem 6.43 (general case). Alternative (recommended): normalization. Over an affine open $U$, $f^{-1}(U)$ is the spectrum of the integral closure of $\Gamma(U)$ in $K$, which is module-finite (a finitely generated $k$-domain is Japanese), of dimension one and integrally closed, hence regular; $X$ is proper because finite over $C$. In the place representation: the centre map $\mathrm{Place}\ k\ K \to C$ is surjective by Chevalley's extension theorem (Mathlib `LocalSubring.exists_le_valuationSubring`, then `TauCeti.Place.ofValuationSubring`), has finite fibres because the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ is module-finite, hence semilocal, and is injective over simple points because a DVR is maximal for domination.
>
> Formalization notes: This is the central decision of the page. Recommended: (i) the points of the model are `TauCeti.Place k K` with the centre map, which is all that Proposition 2, Proposition 3 and Chapter 8 use; (ii) if the model is needed as a curve, take Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.twoChartModel` (any transcendental $x \in K$) or the normalization of $C$; its closed points are the places (`twoChartModel.equivPlace`), and the morphism to $C$ is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.homOfFunctionFieldAlgHom` (source local rings are valuation rings by `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.twoChartModel.valuationRing_stalk`, target proper). Fulton's route (Theorem 2 + Proposition 1) is not needed for existence. How Fulton's "projective curve" is represented is decided on the auxiliary pages; if varieties are schemes, use `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IsCurveOver k`. For a plane curve given by an irreducible form, `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectivePlaneCurve` is the scheme (its properness and dimension one are not yet stated for singular equations). Problem 7.17 is the version for curves that are not projective (then uniqueness needs $f$ finite, or $X$ to be the normalization).

> [!milestone] Nonsingular projective curves correspond to function fields of one variable
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed. $X \mapsto k(X)$ induces a bijection between isomorphism classes of nonsingular projective curves over $k$ and $k$-isomorphism classes of algebraic function fields of one variable over $k$ (finitely generated extensions of transcendence degree one). For nonsingular projective curves $X$, $X'$, dominant morphisms $X' \to X$ correspond bijectively and functorially to $k$-algebra homomorphisms $k(X) \to k(X')$; such morphisms are surjective (Problem 8.18).
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Corollary of Theorem 3] (p. 92), with [@Fulton2008, §7.1, Corollaries 2–3] (p. 82).
>

> [!milestone] Finiteness of the normalization of a plane curve near a point
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C$ an irreducible projective plane curve with nonsingular model $f : X \to C$, $P \in C$, and $S \subseteq C$ finite with $P \notin S$. There is an affine open neighbourhood $U$ of $P$ with $U \cap S = \emptyset$ such that (1) $U' = f^{-1}(U)$ is an affine open subset of $X$; (2) $\Gamma(U')$ is module-finite over $\Gamma(U)$; (3) $t\,\Gamma(U') \subseteq \Gamma(U)$ for some $0 \neq t \in \Gamma(U)$; (4) $\dim_k \Gamma(U')/\Gamma(U) < \infty$. Moreover $\Gamma(f^{-1}(U))$ is the integral closure of $\Gamma(U)$ in $K$ for every open $U \subseteq C$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Lemma 2] (pp. 92–93); the last sentence is [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Problem 7.20 (c)] (p. 94).
>
> Proof routes: Book: compose the neighbourhoods of Step (3) of §7.2 along the quadratic transformations of Theorem 2 and the blow-up of §7.3 (Step (7)), shrinking as needed (Remarks of §7.2, Problem 7.13); module-finiteness is transitive (Problem 1.45); a common denominator gives $t$; $z \mapsto tz$ embeds $\Gamma(U')/\Gamma(U)$ into $\Gamma(U)/(t)$, finite-dimensional because $t$ has finitely many zeros (Corollary 4 of the Nullstellensatz, §1.7). Alternative (recommended, characteristic-free): $\Gamma(U')$ is the integral closure of $\Gamma(U)$ in $K$, i.e. the holomorphy ring of the places centred in $U$; it is module-finite because $\Gamma(U)$ is Japanese; (3) and (4) follow as in the book.
>
> Formalization notes: Localizing (4) at $P$ gives finiteness of $\delta_P = \dim_k \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_P/\mathcal{O}_P(C)$, $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_P$ the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_P(C)$ in $K$; the book does not define $\delta_P$, but it is the standard invariant behind the genus formula $g = \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} - \sum_P \delta_P$ (not in the book; it belongs with Chapter 8). The book's proof goes through Problem 7.13, hence Lemma 1 and characteristic 0. Uses the starred Problem 1.45.
>
> Lean status: Mathlib **related**: `IsIntegralClosure.finite` (Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/IntegralClosure.lean; separable extensions only). Tau Ceti **partial**: `TauCeti.restrictScalars_integralClosure_eq_holomorphyRing` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/HolomorphyRing/Basic.lean) identifies the integral closure of any $k$-subalgebra of $K$ with the holomorphy ring of its places; `TauCeti.Ring.IsUniversallyJapanese.isJapanese` (TauCeti/RingTheory/UniversallyJapanese.lean), `TauCeti.Ring.IsJapanese.finite_integralClosure`, the instance `TauCeti.Ring.IsN1.finite_integralClosure` and `TauCeti.Ring.IsN1.exists_isIntegrallyClosed_localization_away` (TauCeti/RingTheory/Japanese.lean) give (2) and a form of (3) for finitely generated $k$-domains; (4) and the plane-curve statement are not stated.

> [!milestone] Intersection numbers as sums of orders at places
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $C = V(F)$ an irreducible projective plane curve with nonsingular model $f : X \to C$, $P \in C$, and $G$ any projective plane curve (possibly reducible, with multiple components). Let $g$ be the image in $\mathcal{O}_P(C) \subseteq K$ of $G_*$ ($G$ dehomogenized with respect to a line not through $P$) and $\operatorname{ord}_Q(G) = \operatorname{ord}_Q(g)$, independent of the choice. Then
> $$I(P, C \cap G) = \sum_{Q \in f^{-1}(P)} \operatorname{ord}_Q(G),$$
> both sides being $\infty$ exactly when $F \mid G$. More generally (not stated in the book in this form, but it is what the proof shows), for any projective curve $C$ with nonsingular model $f$, $P \in C$ and $0 \neq g \in \mathcal{O}_P(C)$: $\dim_k \mathcal{O}_P(C)/(g) = \sum_{Q \in f^{-1}(P)} \operatorname{ord}_Q(g)$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Proposition 2 and the Notation before it] (p. 93).
>
> Proof routes: Book: $I(P, C\cap G) = \dim_k \mathcal{O}_P(\mathbb{P}^2)/(F_*, G_*) = \dim_k \mathcal{O}_P(C)/(g)$ (Problem 2.44) $= \dim_k \Gamma(U)/(g)$ for $U$ as in Lemma 2 with $g$ a unit at the other points of $U$ (Corollary 1 of §2.9); the index lemma (Problem 2.53) for $\Gamma(U) \subseteq \Gamma(U')$ and multiplication by $g$ gives $\dim \Gamma(U)/(g) = \dim \Gamma(U')/(g)$; by Corollary 1 of §2.9 again and Problem 2.50 this is $\sum_Q \dim \mathcal{O}_Q(X)/(g) = \sum_Q \operatorname{ord}_Q(g)$. Library route: $A = \mathcal{O}_P(C)$, $\widetilde{A}$ its integral closure, the holomorphy ring of the places centred at $P$, a semilocal principal ideal domain; $\widetilde{A}/A$ has finite length, so $\ell(A/gA) = \ell(\widetilde{A}/g\widetilde{A})$ for $g \neq 0$; and $\ell(\widetilde{A}/g\widetilde{A}) = \sum_Q \operatorname{ord}_Q(g)$ by the Chinese remainder theorem (residue fields $k$).
>
> Formalization notes: The general form (any curve, any $g \neq 0$) is the one to formalize; Fulton's follows with the [Chapter 3 page](01-local-properties.md)'s definition of $I$ and Problem 2.44. State the index lemma as a lemma on modules: for an injective endomorphism $T$ of $V$ with $T(W) \subseteq W$ and $V/W$, $W/T(W)$ finite-dimensional, $\dim V/T(V) = \dim W/T(W)$. Watch the junk value of `TauCeti.Place.ord` at $0$ (hypothesis $F \nmid G$). This is the bridge Chapter 8 uses for $\operatorname{div}(G)$ (Proposition 1 of §8.1: $\deg \operatorname{div}(G) = \deg C \cdot \deg G$ with Bézout). Depends on "Finiteness of the normalization of a plane curve near a point" (or on finiteness of the integral closure directly). Uses the starred Problems 2.44, 2.50, 2.53.
>

Tau Ceti **related**: `TauCeti.ord_eq_length_quotient_span_pair` (TauCeti/RingTheory/Intersection.lean) rewrites $\operatorname{length}(R/(f,g))$ as `Ring.ord` of $g$ on $R/(f)$; `TauCeti.isPrincipalIdealRing_holomorphyRing` and `TauCeti.restrictScalars_integralClosure_eq_holomorphyRing` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/HolomorphyRing/Basic.lean); `TauCeti.Place.ord_algebraMap_eq_multiplicity_center` (TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/AffineModel/Place.lean) for Dedekind models. > [!milestone] Noether's conditions at an ordinary multiple point via orders at places
> Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $F$ an irreducible projective plane curve, $P$ an ordinary multiple point of $F$ of multiplicity $r$, and $Q_1, \dots, Q_r$ the places centred at $P$. (Lemma 3) If $z \in K = k(F)$ has $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(z) \geq r - 1$ for $i = 1, \dots, r$, then $z \in \mathcal{O}_P(F)$. (Proposition 3) If $G$, $H$ are plane curves, $F \nmid G$, and $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(H) \geq \operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(G) + r - 1$ for all $i$, then Noether's conditions hold at $P$ for $F, G, H$: $H_* \in (F_*, G_*) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_P(\mathbb{P}^2)$.
>
> Sources: [@Fulton2008, §7.5, Lemma 3 and Proposition 3] (pp. 93–94).
>
> Proof routes: Lemma 3: shrink $U$ so that $z$ is regular at the points of $U$ other than $P$ (the pole set is finite) and $f : U' \to U$ is as in Step (3) of §7.2; $\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}(x) = 1$ (Step (2)) gives $z x^{1-r} \in \Gamma(U')$, and $x^{r-1}\Gamma(U') \subseteq \Gamma(U)$ gives $z \in \Gamma(U) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_P(F)$. Proposition 3: $H_* \in (F_*, G_*)$ iff $H_* \in (G_*)\mathcal{O}_P(F)$ iff $H_*/G_* \in \mathcal{O}_P(F)$; apply Lemma 3.
>
> Formalization notes: Lemma 3 says that the conductor of $\mathcal{O}_P(F)$ in its integral closure contains the $(r-1)$-st power of the Jacobson radical. It needs the identification of the places over $P$ with the points $P_i$ of the blow-up ("Orders at the places over an ordinary multiple point") and the conductor statement of §7.2 Step (3). Noether's conditions and Noether's theorem are on the [Chapter 5 page](02-projective-plane-curves.md). Used in Chapter 8 for the Residue Theorem (§8.1) and in §8.3. For arbitrary singular points the analogous criterion is the theory of adjoints and conductors in [@Fulton2002Adjoints].
>

## Notes on the source

- **Numbering.** In this edition theorems, propositions and lemmas are numbered consecutively through the chapter (Theorem 1 in §7.1, Theorem 2 in §7.4, Theorem 3 in §7.5; Proposition 1 in §7.3, Propositions 2–3 in §7.5; Lemma 1 in §7.4, Lemmas 2–3 in §7.5), corollaries within their theorem, problems as 7.$n$ through the chapter; the book cites them with the section ("Proposition 1 of §7.3"), as does this page. The numbered steps (1)–(8) of §§7.2–7.4 are cited "Step ($n$)". The Corollary of Theorem 3 is unnumbered.
- **Text extraction.** The page was drafted from the text extraction of the PDF; the fact-check compared it with the printed pages (pp. 81–95 and 113, rendered from the PDF). The formulas garbled in the extraction (Problems 7.4, 7.5, 7.20 (b), Steps (5)–(8) of §§7.3–7.4, Proposition 2) read as reconstructed here, up to the printed slips listed below.
- **Problem 7.4 (p. 85), printed slip.** The bracket is printed as $x^{r-1}\bigl(\sum_{j \neq i}(z - \alpha_j) + x + \dots\bigr)$; what is meant (and what the problem's first formula gives) is $x^{r-1}\bigl(\sum_i \prod_{j\neq i}(z - \alpha_j) + x(\dots)\bigr)$.
- **Problem 7.5 (b) (p. 85), printed slip.** The order is printed as $\operatorname{ord}^{C'}_{R_i}(g)$; read $\operatorname{ord}^{C'}_{P_i}(g)$. The problem does not assume that $G$ omits $C$; it is true as stated, since if $F \mid G$ both sides are infinite in (a) and every $L_i$ is tangent to $G$.
- **Corollary 1 of §7.1 (p. 82)** cites "Proposition 11 of §6.5"; Proposition 11 is in §6.6 (p. 77). The proof of Theorem 1 correctly cites Proposition 10 of §6.5.
- **Index slip in §7.3, Steps (5) and (7) (pp. 86–87), confirmed on the printed page.** As printed, the chart is $\varphi(x,z) = [x : xz : 1] \times [1 : z] \times \dots$ (forced by $f_1[x : xz : 1] = [x : xz]$), so its image avoids $V(Y_{11})$, and its inverse is $(x_1/x_3,\ y_{12}/y_{11})$. The text says $V(Y_{12})$, $y_{11}/y_{12}$ and "$Q$ corresponds to $[\lambda : 1]$"; the consistent version is $V(Y_{11})$, $y_{12}/y_{11}$, $[1 : \lambda]$. The inverse is also written with projective brackets for a point of $\mathbb{A}^2$. The argument is unaffected.
- **Problem 7.20 (p. 94).** The integral closure is printed as $\{z \in k \mid \dots\}$ (lower-case $k$, checked on the printed page); read $z \in K$. Part (b) is meant for domains $R_\alpha$ with common fraction field $K$: if each is integrally closed, so is $\bigcap R_\alpha$.

- **Step (3) of §7.2** uses the normalization of the Assumption (coefficient of $Y^r$ equal to $1$) to get $H(0,0) = 1$.
- **Steps (1)–(4) of §7.4** assume $C$ is not an exceptional line (stated before Step (1)); $(F')' = F$ holds exactly, with no constant: the exponents of $X$, $Y$, $Z$ in $(F^Q)^Q = (XYZ)^n F$ all come out to $n$.
- **Step (7) (b) of §7.4 (p. 90), confirmed on the printed page.** When $n - r \leq 1$ the points $P'$, $P''$ are simple points of $C'$ or not on $C'$ (and $P$ is simple when $n = 1$), although the text calls them ordinary multiple points of multiplicities $n$, $n-r$, $n-r$.
- **Characteristic.** Lemma 1 is false in characteristic $p$ (Appendix A); Theorem 2's characteristic-$p$ proof is only sketched there and rests on "at most one terrible point", proved through the dual curve (Problem 6.47). In that sketch the auxiliary centre $Q$ must itself be non-terrible, and "$p$ doesn't divide $n - r$" should read $n' - r$. The book does not say how Problems 7.13 and 7.21 (a), (c) (which use Lemma 1) and the proof of Lemma 2 (which uses 7.13) go in characteristic $p$; the drafting agent believed the Appendix A trick extends to them; the fact-check did not confirm this, so it remains open. The normalization route recommended here makes Lemma 2, Theorem 3 and Proposition 2 characteristic-free.
- **Surjectivity in Theorem 3.** For non-plane $C$ the book uses Problem 6.43, whose hint (Problem 6.32) is in the characteristic-0 setting of Problem 6.31. Surjectivity is anyway automatic: a dominant morphism of projective curves is surjective (Problem 8.18), or, for places, by Chevalley's extension theorem.
- **No δ-invariant.** The book defines neither $\delta_P$ nor the genus formula with $\delta_P$; the closest are Lemma 2 (4) and Problems 7.15–7.16 (neighbouring singularities). The local form of Noether's formula on this page is not stated in the book.
- **Problems left to the reader.** Steps (3), (4) of §7.3 (Problems 7.7, 7.8), part of Step (5) of §7.4 ("we leave the rest to the reader"), and the morphism checks of §§7.2–7.3 ("we will leave it to the reader to verify that they are morphisms") are not proved in the text.

## Deferred

- **Problem 7.22 (Plücker formula).** $i + 6\delta + 8\kappa = 3n(n-2)$ for a curve with only ordinary flexes, simple nodes and ordinary cusps. It is a standard result, but it rests on the Hessian and flex theory of Problems 5.23–5.24, implicitly assumes characteristic 0 (flexes and the Hessian behave differently in small characteristic), and nothing later uses it. Recommended as an optional extension after Chapter 8.
- **Fulton's explicit blow-up $B \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2 \times (\mathbb{P}^1)^t$** (Steps (1)–(8) of §7.3, Problems 7.7–7.9) as a *route*: Proposition 1 stays a milestone, but its recommended proof is through the nonsingular model, so formalizing the multiprojective construction is optional.
- **Worked examples** (Problems 7.2, 7.6, 7.10–7.12): not milestones; useful as tests of the definitions.
